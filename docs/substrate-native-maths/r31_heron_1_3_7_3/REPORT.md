## §1 Heron's structure (attested)

Heron of Alexandria's *Mechanica* (in 3 books) + *Pneumatica* (in 2 books, ~78 device descriptions) is the canonical engineering text of late antiquity. Per Drachmann 1948 + Cuomo 2001 + Tybjerg 2003:

### The 1 — Lever-principle (foundational simple machine)

Heron *Mechanica* II.1-32: the **lever** is the foundational mechanical-advantage principle. Per Heron, every other simple machine reduces to or composes with the lever. Heron explicitly cites Archimedes' lever-principle as the foundational anchor.

**A-N**: Class A. (a) at this slot.

### The 3 — Three force-redirector simple machines

Heron canonically classifies 5 simple machines: lever / wheel-and-axle / pulley / wedge / screw. Among these, three are "force-redirectors" (vs lever which is the foundational principle, and screw which is a composite of inclined-plane-around-cylinder):

- **Wheel-and-axle** — radial force redirection
- **Pulley** — directional force redirection (with mechanical advantage via multiple pulleys)
- **Wedge** — concentrated force redirection

This is a candidate 3-slot reading: "lever (1) + three force-redirectors (3) + screw (composite) + ?" — but it's not the canonical Heron partition.

**A-N**: I + C + J. (b) at this slot — present but not canonically partitioned as "3 force-redirectors."

### The +3 — Three pneumatic apparatus families

*Pneumatica* organizes devices into three primary apparatus-families per Schmidt 1899 ed + Woodcroft 1851 trans:

1. **Water-clocks + water-organs** (timekeeping + sound-production apparatus)
2. **Dispensers + automata** (vending + temple-mechanism apparatus)
3. **Engines + force-amplifiers** (steam aeolipile + siphon + force-pump apparatus)

These are explicitly meta-categories of apparatus (not apparatus themselves). Direct match for R30 +3 = projection-enablers.
