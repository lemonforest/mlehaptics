## §1 — How the nudibranch does it (mechanism walk-through)

The kleptocnidae pipeline is well-documented at the morphological + cellular level across
several decades of work. Six PMC-extracted papers anchor the operational chain:

### §1.1 Anatomy of the cnidosac

**Goodheart et al. 2018** ([10.1186/s12983-018-0289-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12983-018-0289-2),
PMC6234619, *Front Zool* 15:43): the cnidosac is *"a muscular prolongation of the digestive gland
located in the apex of each ceras."* Each ceras (dorsal finger-like outgrowth; ~10-100 per
nudibranch depending on species) terminates in a cnidosac. The cerata project the cnidosacs
into a regular array on the dorsal surface — the "skin" where the user's intuition lands.

The cnidosac itself has **three functional zones** per Ekimova et al. 2022
([10.1186/s12983-022-00474-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12983-022-00474-9), PMC9670572, *Front Zool* 19:29):
*"the proliferation zone, the cnidophage zone, and the cnidopore zone."*

- **Proliferation zone** — small epithelial cells migrate distally as they enlarge
- **Cnidophage zone** — specialized cells (cnidophages) that engulf and store nematocysts
- **Cnidopore zone** — apical chamber + exit pore for nematocyst deployment

### §1.2 Pipeline from cnidarian prey to deployment

**Step 1 — Ingestion without triggering**. Greenwood 2009
([10.1016/j.toxicon.2009.02.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.toxicon.2009.02.029), PMC2783962,
*Toxicon* 54:1065) identifies multiple protective mechanisms:
- *"A hard chitin cuticle lining the buccal cavity and esophagus prevents nematocyst penetration"* — physical barrier (substrate-level Class L: graph topology of gut wall)
- *"Epithelial spindles"* — intracellular chitinous discs in mucus-containing cells that absorb discharge impact
- *"The predator's mucus chemically suppresses or physically insulates nematocysts from cnidarian receptor apparatus"* — chemical inhibition

**Step 2 — Branched digestive gland routing**. The digestive gland branches into the cerata
(diverticula). *Some* unfired nematocysts pass through this branching network and arrive at
the proliferation zone of each cnidosac. Per Wikipedia/Kleptocnidy (citing primary literature):
*"nudibranchs feature branched digestive glands connecting to cerata-tip cnidosacs."*

**Step 3 — Cnidophage engulfment**. Per Goodheart et al. 2018: cnidophages are epithelial
cells in the cnidosac middle zone that *"incorporate kleptocnides into vacuoles."* The
membrane-encased storage is critical: nematocysts *"do not discharge if the cnidophage
membrane does not rupture upon release"* (Wikipedia/Kleptocnidy synthesis).

**Step 4 — Maturation within the cnidosac** (Frick 1984 Spurilla studies cite-by-ref;
Greenwood 2009 PMC2783962 summary): *"immature nematocysts, which are incapable of discharge,
are incorporated into cnidosacs where they complete their maturation."* The nudibranch hosts
the development. Per Goodheart et al. 2018: *"Nematocysts are not functional when taken up by
the cnidophages but mature via proton transport within the cnidosac."* Proton transport →
acidification → matrix polymerization → discharge-readiness.

**Step 5 — Threat deployment**. When the nudibranch is bitten / touched by a predator, the
cnidosac musculature (1.5-9 μm thick layers per Ekimova et al. 2022) contracts, the cnidopore
opens, the cnidophage ruptures, and mature nematocysts discharge into the attacker. The
nudibranch's own skin is unharmed because the nematocysts are inside the cnidophage's
membrane until threat-triggered release.

## §2 — The differentiator cascade (user's explicit ask)

> *"what cascade make it different from other seaslugs"*

The question has a clean answer once §1 is mapped. The differentiator IS the **D∘C
diverticulum-to-cnidosac routing** operation — a Class D dispatch composed with a Class C
cascade-orientation on the digestive-gland morphology — which is morphologically present in
aeolids and absent (or vestigial) in the comparison groups.

### §2.1 Aeolid nudibranchs vs dorid nudibranchs

**Dorids** (the other major nudibranch clade — Doridoidea) have **dorsally smooth bodies**
without cerata. They use a fundamentally different defense cascade.

**Cimino et al. 1983** ([10.1126/science.219.4589.1237](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.219.4589.1237),
PubMed 17771309, *Science* 219:1237) established that the dorid *Dendrodoris limbata*
*"elaborates polygodial, a sesquiterpenoid dialdehyde stored in the mantle"* via **de novo
biosynthesis**: *"A biosynthetic experiment with mevalonic acid labeled with carbon-14 showed
that the nudibranch...elaborates polygodial."* This is *not* sequestration. Other dorids
*do* sequester sponge-derived metabolites, but the storage compartment is the **mantle
(epidermis)**, not specialized cerata-tip vacuoles.

**Differentiator class chain**:
- Aeolids: **M ∘ D ∘ C** (HDC self/non-self bind for foreign organelles, dispatch to
  diverticulum, cascade to cnidosac) for cnidocyte ingestion + storage
- Dorids: **F ∘ catalog-A** (template / biosynthetic-pathway render of a stored metabolite
  recipe, content-addressed by enzyme cascade) for de novo terpenoid production

The presence of Class M in aeolids (foreign-organelle handling — analogous to immune-system
tolerance) IS the kleptocnidae-enabling primitive. Dorids don't have it because they don't
handle foreign organelles; they handle foreign *molecules* (sequestered or synthesized) via
mantle storage with no special membrane-discipline cascade.

### §2.2 Aeolid nudibranchs vs sacoglossan sea slugs (kleptoplasty sister)

**Sacoglossans** (sister kleptotrophic strategy — chloroplast theft from green algae) provide
a striking same-cascade-different-organelle comparison. Per Cruz & Cartaxana 2022
([10.1371/journal.pbio.3001857](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3001857), PMC9642861,
*PLoS Biol* 20:e3001857): in *Elysia chlorotica* and related kleptoplastic species,
*"macroalgal chloroplasts are phagocytized by cells of the digestive tubules, which ramify
throughout most of the sea slug's body."*

The cascade SHAPE is the same — gut→branched diverticula→cellular storage with foreign-
organelle preservation — but the organelle differs (chloroplast vs nematocyst), the storage
cell differs (digestive-tubule cell vs cnidophage), and the function differs (photosynthesis
vs defense).

**Same cascade**: M ∘ D ∘ C (foreign-organelle bind / dispatch through diverticula / cascade-
orient toward storage cells).

**Different sub-operation**:
- Aeolids' cascade terminates in a **discrete-deployment apparatus** (cnidosac → cnidopore →
  rupture-on-touch). This is Class **K**: an asymptotic-DOF / pin-slot threshold mechanism
  — the cnidosac stays loaded (DOF accumulated) until predator-bite triggers discharge.
- Sacoglossans' cascade terminates in **continuous-metabolic exploitation** (chloroplast
  performs photosynthesis steadily, sometimes for months — *Elysia chlorotica* retains
  function up to 10 months per Cruz 2022). This is more Class **L** continuous-eigenoperation
  (sustained photochemical eigenstate) than Class K threshold.

So the differentiator within "theft and repurpose" between aeolids and sacoglossans is the
**K vs L terminal sub-operation** — discrete-threshold deployment vs continuous-eigenoperation.
Same M ∘ D ∘ C prefix; different K (defense) vs L (metabolism) terminus.

### §2.3 Aeolid nudibranchs vs other cladobranch families that LOST kleptocnidae

Per Ekimova et al. 2022 and Goodheart et al. 2018: kleptocnidae has been **lost at least
4 times** within Cladobranchia (Phyllodesmium, Fionidae, Favorinus, Bulbaeolidia).

**Phyllodesmium** is particularly instructive. This genus feeds on octocorals (soft corals)
that have *Symbiodinium* zooxanthellae symbionts. Phyllodesmium has *abandoned* the
kleptocnidae cascade and instead developed kleptoplasty-like retention of the zooxanthellae
themselves (functional equivalent of the sacoglossan pathway). Same M ∘ D ∘ C prefix,
switched terminal from K (cnidosac deployment) to L (continuous photosymbiosis).

**Fionidae** has lost kleptocnidae three times within the family per Ekimova et al. 2022
(*Phestilla*, *Calma*, *Fiona* lineages). *Phestilla* (corallivore on hard corals) +
*Fiona* (open-ocean drifter) both kept the cerata morphology but the cnidosacs are
non-functional. The **M ∘ D ∘ C prefix is preserved** (diverticula still go to cerata) but the
terminal Class K saturation/deployment apparatus has degenerated.

This loss-pattern attests cleanly to the cascade structure: the K terminal is independently
gain-able and loss-able while the M ∘ D ∘ C prefix is conserved.

### §2.4 Twice-stolen nematocysts (recently documented chain)

**Krupitskaya, Ekimova & Malakhov 2025** (DOI [10.1134/S0012496624600295](https://doi.org/10.1134/S0012496624600295),
*Dokl Biol Sci* 520:1, cite-by-ref Springer) documented *Coryphella trophina* eating *other
nudibranchs* and incorporating their already-stolen nematocysts: *"stinging capsules...have
been stolen twice: first by mollusks feeding on cnidarians and then by C. trophina eating the
mollusks."*

This is the **cascade-composition recursion** — class chain operating on its own output. The
M ∘ D ∘ C ∘ K chain that originally took nematocysts from cnidarians is fed nematocysts that
were ALREADY taken by another aeolid; the second nudibranch's cascade processes them
identically. Confirms that the cascade is operating on the *byte-shape* of nematocysts, not on
their source-context. This is the same composability property as cascade-on-circles per
`[[user_stance_cascade_lives_on_circles]]`.

## §3 — Selectivity discipline (Class E catalog lookup attestation)

The Class E catalog lookup operation in §1.3 needs its strongest evidence. The newest +
strongest selectivity datum:

**Dror, Lotan & Angel 2025** ([10.1186/s12983-025-00589-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12983-025-00589-9),
PMC12590869, *Front Zool* 22): *Caloria militaris* (an alien aeolid in the eastern
Mediterranean) was tracked feeding on scyphozoan polyps (4 jellyfish species) and hydrozoan
polyps. Quantitative result: across 258 days of feeding on scyphozoans, **zero** nematocysts
incorporated from scyphozoan prey. When feeding on the hydrozoan *Pennaria disticha*,
nematocysts incorporated within 3-6 days. *"The first report to demonstrate complete
selectivity in nematocyst sequestration over an extended feeding period."*

Within the sequestered set from *P. disticha*, the proportions DIFFERED significantly from
the prey's own nematocyst distribution (PERMANOVA R² = 0.56, p < 0.0001):
- Desmonemes: 35.9% (over-represented)
- Large stenoteles: 19.0%
- Small stenoteles: 18.2%
- Small mastigophores: 13.3%
- Medium stenoteles: 11.9%
- Large mastigophores: 1.5% (under-represented vs source)

This is Class E in full force: a **sorted-key lookup discipline** on nematocyst type that
selectively admits desmonemes + stenoteles + small mastigophores while excluding large
mastigophores AND completely rejecting scyphozoan nematocyst types. The lookup key is
some molecular signature on the nematocyst capsule (likely glycoprotein) that the
proliferation-zone cells discriminate against.

Mechanism is unknown but the selectivity is empirical and clean. Math-doesn't-lie: Class E
catalog discipline operates on nematocyst types regardless of how it's implemented at the
molecular level.

## §4 — Wider biological "theft and repurpose" — future-spike fermatas

The kleptocnidae cascade-shape is repeated across biology. Each is a candidate future spike
per `[[user_stance_cross_substrate_cascade_matching_as_research_method]]`. Quick survey:

### §4.1 Monarch butterflies → milkweed cardenolides

**Agrawal et al. 2021** PNAS 118:e2024463118 (PMC8072370): monarch *Danaus plexippus* larvae
ingest milkweed (*Asclepias* spp.) and sequester cardenolides (cardiac glycosides) from leaf
tissue. Resistance to these heart-poisons evolved via stepwise Na+/K+-ATPase mutations
(Petschenka et al. 2013 *Evolution*). The sequestered cardenolides become the adult's
predator defense.

**Cascade shape**: M (self/non-self bind at gut wall — cardenolides not attacked as foreign)
∘ D (dispatch to hemolymph for whole-body distribution) ∘ E (selective sequestration — 40% of
leaf cardenolides, voruscharin, NOT kept; metabolized instead) ∘ K (storage-until-attack
deployment, but distributed rather than discrete cnidosacs).

**Differentiator from kleptocnidae**: storage substrate is **molecular** (free cardenolide
in hemolymph + cuticle) not **organellar**. No Class C cascade-orientation through anatomical
diverticula because the storage is fluid-distributed. Sub-cascade is M ∘ D ∘ E ∘ K with
Class L (graph topology) replacing Class C (anatomical cascade-orientation) as the
distribution substrate.

### §4.2 Poison dart frogs → arthropod alkaloids

**Daly et al. 1994** + decades of follow-up: dendrobatid frogs sequester over 900
alkaloids from dietary arthropods (ants, mites, centipedes). Skin storage in dorsal
granular glands. Uptake within 4 days per recent work.

**Cascade shape**: M ∘ D ∘ E (alkaloid-type selectivity — frogs collected from different
arthropod-diet regions have different alkaloid profiles, but each frog admits only a subset)
∘ K (skin gland deployment-on-attack).

**Differentiator from kleptocnidae**: same prefix M ∘ D ∘ E ∘ K but Class L distribution
substrate (vascular → skin glands) rather than Class C cascade-orientation. Storage is
**organ-level discrete** (granular skin glands) rather than **cell-level discrete**
(cnidophages); the discreteness scale differs but Class K threshold-deployment is the same.

### §4.3 Sequestering newts → tetrodotoxin

Newts (Taricha) sequester (or possibly host symbionts that produce) tetrodotoxin from
dietary bacteria. Toll-like defense via skin / liver storage.

**Cascade shape**: candidate for same M ∘ D ∘ E ∘ K but with possible Class L sub-operation
(symbiont/host network rather than pure ingestion). Future spike candidate; literature
murkier on biosynthesis-vs-sequestration question.

### §4.4 Comb jellies (Haeckelia) → narcomedusan nematocysts

Per Greenwood 2009 review + Wikipedia/Kleptocnidy: one ctenophore genus, *Haeckelia*,
sequesters cnidarian nematocysts and lines them up its tentacles. *Convergent evolution*
with aeolid kleptocnidae — different phylum (Ctenophora), same end-state.

**Cascade shape**: full M ∘ D ∘ C ∘ K chain by direct convergence. Tentacular canal replaces
ceras diverticulum; tentacle surface replaces cnidosac; the operations chain is structurally
identical. This is direct evidence that the M ∘ D ∘ C ∘ K cascade is the *minimum sufficient
operation set* for nematocyst-theft-and-repurpose — convergent evolution selects for the
same algebraic shape.

### §4.5 Flatworm kleptocnidae (Acoelomorpha + Platyhelminthes)

Per Greenwood 2009 + Wikipedia/Kleptocnidy: flatworms have evolved kleptocnidae **6-13
independent times** across multiple orders (Catenulida, Macrostomorpha, Proseriata,
Prolecithophora, Polycladida, Rhabdocoela). Storage in epidermal cyst cells.

The 6-13× independent-evolution count is a strong signal: it indicates the M ∘ D ∘ C ∘ K
cascade has a **low effective edit distance** from generic predatory-flatworm body plan.
The substrate doesn't need much modification to support the cascade. Compare with aeolid
single (or double) origin and ctenophore single origin per Goodheart & Bely 2017
(cite-by-ref *Invert Biol* 136:75).

Future-spike candidate: comparative cnidosac/cyst-cell **L+M** algebra across phyla. If the
graph-topology + HDC operations partition cleanly along phylogenetic lines, the cascade-
match has substrate-independent invariants.
