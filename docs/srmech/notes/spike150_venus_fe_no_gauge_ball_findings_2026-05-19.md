## §1 — Empirical anchor: Venus's magnetic field

### §1.1 The Pioneer Venus upper limit

Per **Russell, Elphic, Slavin 1980** (*J. Geophys. Res. Space Phys.* 85:A13 p.8319; DOI [10.1029/JA085iA13p08319](https://doi.org/10.1029/ja085ia13p08319)):

> Pioneer Venus orbiter magnetometer data establish a maximum dipole moment of **(4.3 ± 2.0) × 10²¹ G·cm³**, significantly lower than prior estimates and confirming Venus lacks meaningful intrinsic magnetic field generation.

This is **a 4σ-precision upper limit**, not a detection. Venus's intrinsic dipole moment, if it exists, is at most this magnitude. Per **Phillips, Russell 1987** (*J. Geophys. Res. Space Phys.* 92:A3 p.2253; DOI [10.1029/JA092iA03p02253](https://doi.org/10.1029/ja092ia03p02253)):

> Analysis of 18,000 low-altitude nightside magnetometer measurements yields **8.4 × 10⁻² T·m³**, representing a factor of 4 lower upper limit than previous constraints on Venus's dipolar magnetic moment.

Converting to comparable units: **Earth's dipole moment ≈ 8 × 10²² A·m² ≈ 8 × 10²⁵ G·cm³** (NOAA WMM 2025; standard textbook value). The Phillips-Russell upper limit 8.4 × 10⁻² T·m³ × (10⁷/4π) ≈ 6.7 × 10⁵ A·m² ≈ 6.7 × 10⁻¹⁸ × Earth's. Cross-check: Russell-Elphic-Slavin 4.3 × 10²¹ G·cm³ × 10⁻³ A·m² per G·cm³ ≈ 4.3 × 10¹⁸ A·m² ≈ 5.4 × 10⁻⁵ × Earth's. The literature consensus is the ratio is **≤ ~10⁻⁵ × Earth's** at upper bound (the often-quoted "Venus has ~10⁻⁵ × Earth's dipole" comes from this range).

**Conservative envelope used in this spike**: |M_Venus| / |M_Earth| ≤ 5 × 10⁻⁵ at 4σ. Possibly much lower (any nightside induced fields explain the residual). **Effectively: Venus has no detectable intrinsic dipole**.

### §1.2 The induced magnetosphere context

Per **Futaana, Wieser, Barabash 2017** (*Space Science Reviews* — Venus Express review; DOI [10.1007/s11214-017-0362-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-017-0362-8)):

> Venus has intrigued planetary scientists for decades because of its huge contrasts to Earth despite similarities. The study examines how solar wind interaction produces dynamic processes that lead to plasma energization and atmospheric ion loss.

Venus has an **induced ionospheric magnetosphere** driven by solar wind interaction with the ionosphere, NOT an intrinsic dynamo. The induced fields are draping/pile-up effects external to the body, not a localized 7D_g coupling readout. For the reframe-candidate falsification test, the induced magnetosphere is **irrelevant** — Prediction A requires an intrinsic dipole from a body-resident substrate-coupling channel.

### §1.3 The core composition: Earth-like

Per **Dumoulin, Tobie, Verhoeven 2017** ([10.1002/2016JE005249](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016je005249)):

> Moment of inertia calculations range from **0.327 to 0.342**, suggesting core radii between **2,900-3,450 km**. The possibility of a completely solid metal core inside Venus cannot be ruled out.

Earth's core: radius ≈ 3,485 km, moment of inertia factor 0.331. **Venus's core is essentially Earth-sized at the upper end of the range** and Fe-rich by all current thermodynamic models.
