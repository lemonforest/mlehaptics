## SUB-TASK B — Dark-sector non-uniformity measurement methodology

### B.1 The user's challenge — sharpened

Standard cosmology (ΛCDM) assumes dark-matter distribution is *smooth* at scales > ~few Mpc; fine structure is unresolved and inferred via N-body simulations. The user asks: why is uniformity assumed? Earth-scale gravity field IS mapped to milligal precision via geodetic surveys; why can't dark-sector content near AGN-gas-cloud systems be similarly mapped?

### B.2 Literature scan (TOS-compliant sources)

#### B.2.1 Dark matter density measurement resolution limits

**Galactic-scale** (Mpc / sub-Mpc):
- **N-body cosmological simulations** (Springel-Frenk-White 2005 Millennium Run; arXiv:astro-ph/0504097 cite-by-ref): resolves substructure to ~10⁸ M_sun halo scale; below that, particle-noise-dominated.
- **Subhalo mass function from Aquarius** (Springel et al. 2008, arXiv:0809.0898 cite-by-ref): predicts ~10⁵ subhalos in MW-mass halo above 10⁴ M_sun.
- **Lensing substructure detection limit** (Vegetti+ 2012, arXiv:1201.3643 cite-by-ref): smallest detected dark substructure ~10⁹ M_sun via strong-lensing flux anomaly in SDSS J0946+1006.

**Resolution gap**: simulations predict 10⁴–10⁵ M_sun substructures; observations resolve ~10⁹ M_sun via strong lensing; this 4–5 OOM gap is the **dark-sector resolution wall**.

#### B.2.2 AGN-vicinity gravitational measurements

**Sgr A* (M = 4.297 × 10⁶ M_sun)**:
- **S-star orbits** (Gillessen et al. 2009 arXiv:0810.4674 cite-by-ref + Gravity Collab 2018 arXiv:1807.09409 cite-by-ref): orbit fitting constrains central mass to ~10⁻³ relative precision via Keplerian + GR fit.
- **GRAVITY interferometer 2018 detection of Schwarzschild precession of S2**: arXiv:1807.09409 — bit-precision orbital reconstruction in AGN vicinity; sensitivity to enclosed-mass at 10⁻⁴ relative precision within S2's orbit.
- **EHT M87* observation** (EHT Collab 2019 arXiv:1906.11242 PDF-verified per Spike #108): mass = (6.5 ± 0.7) × 10⁹ M_sun; ring photon ring resolves to ~10 r_g spatial scale.

**Key observational fact**: in AGN-vicinity, gravitational mass is measured to ~10⁻³ relative precision; far better than the cosmological-density uniformity assumption.

#### B.2.3 Strong-lensing-based fine-resolution dark-sector mapping

- **Vegetti+ 2010, arXiv:1002.4708 (cite-by-ref)**: detection of dark substructure at 3.5 × 10⁹ M_sun in B1938+666 lens.
- **Hezaveh+ 2016, arXiv:1601.01388 (cite-by-ref)**: 10⁹ M_sun detection in ALMA observations of SDP.81.
- **Dalal-Kochanek 2002 (cite-by-ref)**: flux-ratio statistics constrain substructure abundance.
- **Ostdiek+ 2022, arXiv:2207.06845 (cite-by-ref)**: ML-based substructure detection in HST snapshots; sensitivity to ~10⁸ M_sun subhalos.
- **CLASH multi-cluster strong-lensing** (Postman+ 2012, arXiv:1106.3328 cite-by-ref): cluster-scale dark-matter mapping at ~kpc resolution.

#### B.2.4 Hot-gas X-ray constraints on dark-sector around clusters

- **Vikhlinin+ 2006 (arXiv:astro-ph/0507092 cite-by-ref)** + **Pratt+ 2009 (arXiv:0809.3784 cite-by-ref)**: X-ray surface-brightness modelling constrains cluster mass profiles via hydrostatic equilibrium of hot intracluster medium (ICM).
- **Method**: gas pressure balance P_gas + P_DM_dispersion = local-gravity ∫M(<r)/r² dr; deviations show *non-uniform* dark-matter distribution at sub-cluster scales.
- **Resolution**: ~few-kpc inside cluster cores; consistent with bumpy NFW-like profiles, not smooth.

#### B.2.5 Pulsar timing arrays — fine-scale gravitational gradient detection

- **NANOGrav 15-year data** (arXiv:2306.16213 cite-by-ref): detected stochastic gravitational-wave background; sensitivity to dark-sector-density gradients via pulsar-timing-residual cross-correlations.
- **Pulsar dispersion measure (DM) variations** track local electron column density; combined with timing, constrain intervening compact mass distributions.
- **Compact dark object microlensing** (Niikura+ 2019 arXiv:1701.02151 cite-by-ref): Subaru HSC observations of M31 constrain primordial-BH dark-matter fraction to <few% in 10⁻¹¹–10⁻⁶ M_sun range.

#### B.2.6 Weak-lensing tomography — wide-field dark-sector mapping

- **DES Y3 cosmic shear** (arXiv:2105.13549 cite-by-ref): constrains dark-matter clustering amplitude S_8 to ~3%.
- **KiDS-1000 cosmic shear** (Asgari+ 2021 arXiv:2007.15633 cite-by-ref): cross-validates DES.
- **Resolution**: arcmin-scale projected dark-matter maps; sufficient for sheet-vs-lump distinction at cluster-cluster filament scale (~Mpc).

## SUB-TASK C — Metal-mountain-as-capacitor geodetic test

### C.1 Standard geodesy framing

Gravity anomalies in mountainous regions are interpreted via **Bouguer correction** (Bouguer 1749) + **isostatic correction** (Airy-Heiskanen / Pratt-Hayford models):

- Free-air anomaly: g_obs − g_theoretical (latitude-corrected)
- Bouguer anomaly: free-air − attraction of topographic mass (assumed crustal density 2670 kg/m³)
- Isostatic anomaly: Bouguer − compensation effect

**Result**: residuals are attributed to **density excess** (denser rocks → positive Bouguer anomaly) or **density deficit** (less dense → negative). For metal-rich mountains, the standard interpretation is purely mass-density-driven.

### C.2 Literature scan — major iron-rich gravity anomalies

| Region | Anomaly magnitude | Attributed cause | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kursk Magnetic Anomaly** (Russia) | +60 mGal Bouguer | Banded iron formation (BIF); reduced-density host vs Fe-rich dome | Pevzner+ 1991 (cite-by-ref) |
| **Sudbury Basin Ni-deposit** (Canada) | +30 mGal | Density-excess from impact-melt sulfide complex | Wu+ 1995 (cite-by-ref) |
| **Witwatersrand Au-bearing region** (S. Africa) | +20 mGal | Quartzite + minor pyrite cementation | Lindeque+ 2007 (cite-by-ref) |
| **Carajás iron province** (Brazil) | +50 mGal | Hematite + magnetite deposits | Pinheiro & Holdsworth 2000 (cite-by-ref) |
| **Pilbara BIF** (Australia) | +40 mGal | Iron ore + jaspilite | Goodwin 1962 (cite-by-ref) |
| **Bouguer of Andes** (general) | strongly negative (−400 mGal at high elevations) | Crustal thickening + isostatic compensation | Fukao+ 1989 (cite-by-ref) |

**Critical observation from the literature**: **gravity anomalies over metal-rich provinces ARE positive (excess), consistent with density-excess interpretation, BUT**:

1. Anomaly *magnitudes* are consistent with calculated density-excess from known ore body extent + standard rock densities.
2. **Residuals beyond density-excess models do exist** — see Bouguer-anomaly modeling literature (e.g., Mickus & Hinojosa 2001 cite-by-ref); these are typically attributed to deep crustal density structure or measurement uncertainty.
3. **No published anomaly is attributed to electromagnetic or capacitor effects**.

