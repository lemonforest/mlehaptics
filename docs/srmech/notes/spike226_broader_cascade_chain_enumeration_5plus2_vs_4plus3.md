**Definition A**: a cascade-chain T : (cascade-class composition) is a **structural-substrate equivalence** between the (4,3) framework Hopf-bundle partition and the (5,2) Chinese suanpan mechanical partition iff:

3. **Class C orientation preserved**: no orientation flip in cascade-direction (per `[[user_stance_kepler_shape_universal]]` form-IS-function — cascade IS operation regardless of substrate; orientation flip would be a different cascade-shape).
4. **Class L graph-structure topology preserved**: linear-chain positional cascade topology preserved (no insertion / deletion of cascade nodes).

This is the PRIMARY equivalence definition tested by the enumeration. The partition-reassignment in (2) is the operational signature of (4,3) → (5,2) transformation under conditions that match Spike-research #225 §2.4 Q4 setup.

### §1.2 — Definition B: Computational-outcome equivalence (NOTED, NOT TESTED)

**Definition B**: a cascade-chain T : (cascade-class composition) is a **computational-outcome equivalence** between 5+2 suanpan arithmetic and 4+3 framework gauge-coupling iff they produce the same observable outcomes for some operationally-defined test problem.

Definition B is noted as an open extension (future-spike candidate) but is NOT TESTED here. The verdict scope is bounded to Definition A.

### §1.3 — Equivalence as structural identity not computational analogy

Per `[[user_stance_identity_not_implementation_discipline]]`: equivalence under Definition A IS structural identity, not implementational analogy. If a cascade-chain T satisfies the four invariants in §1.1, then (4,3) and (5,2) ARE the same partition viewed through different projection lens — same identity. **Class J canonical operation**: factor an integer N into its prime constituents. For N = 7 (the cardinality of the substrate):

- 7 is prime
- Prime factorisation: 7 = 7^1 (a single prime factor of multiplicity 1)
- Class J's canonical output: {(7, 1)} — the single prime factor

**Additive partitions of 7** (the structures under comparison):

- (4, 3): 4 + 3 = 7
- (5, 2): 5 + 2 = 7
- (6, 1): 6 + 1 = 7
- (3, 4): 3 + 4 = 7 (rearrangement of (4,3))
- (2, 5): (rearrangement of (5,2))
- ... etc.

**Critical structural observation**: Class J's canonical multiplicative-factorisation operation does NOT produce additive partitions of 7.
