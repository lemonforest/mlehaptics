3. **3D ↔ 7D inverse ontology.** This is the sharpest claim. **"Where there are not real but very consequential things in 3D, it is probably the inverse in 7D."**

   *Non-real-but-consequential in 3D* — observable examples to start a catalog:
   - Virtual particles (consequential through Casimir, Lamb shift, vacuum polarization; not real as individual events).
   - Gauge degrees of freedom (consequential through Aharonov-Bohm; not real as gauge-variant observables).
   - Statistical regularities (consequential for thermodynamics, fluid dynamics; not real as individual microstates).
   - Wavefunction amplitudes (consequential for probabilities; not real as direct measurements).
   - Expectation values (consequential for QFT, statistical mechanics; not real as single observations).
   - Symmetry constraints (consequential as conservation laws; not real as direct objects).

   *Inverse-in-7D* (the conjecture) — what would the inverse look like?
   - **Reading A:** things that are *real but inconsequential* (real values of compactified moduli that don't affect 3D physics directly). Symmetric-opposite of "consequential-but-not-real."
   - **Reading B:** things that are *real and consequential at 7D* but appear *non-real and consequential at 3D under projection*. I.e., the same ontological content appears under different aspects depending on which dimensional subspace you observe it from. This is the *dual* reading.
   - **Reading C:** something else entirely. The "inverse" might be a different relation — perhaps reciprocal, perhaps complementary, perhaps duality-of-rôles.

   The concertmaster's job is to operationalize "inverse" precisely enough to test which reading (A, B, C, or other) is consistent with both project framings and observable physics.

4. **"Sum the real from all dims to find 1D and 11D structure."** The reconstruction recipe: integrate / sum / aggregate the *real* contributions across all dimensional subspaces (3D + 7D + 1D) to recover the 1D-compressed-equivalent-of-11D total content.
