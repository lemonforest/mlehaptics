**The question (user, 2026-06-03):** "Are ganglia not just smaller synaptic-neural networks?"

## Verdict — yes, and it is the strong form of brain-agnostic (F118)
Neural tissue is the **same KIND of structure** (neurons + synaptic couplings — the synaptic-neural network of F311) at **every aggregation scale**:
```
   diffuse nerve net  →  ganglion  →  ganglionic chain  →  centralized brain
       (Hydra)           (Aplysia)        (insects)          (vertebrates)
```
The **brain is not a privileged object** — it is a *large, dense* synaptic-neural network. A **ganglion is a small one**; a **nerve net** a diffuse one; a **rhopalium** a sensory-specialized one. There is **no privileged neural aggregation** — this is the **no-privileged-X discipline** (no privileged primitive class `[[feedback_no_privileged_primitive_classes]]`; no privileged model; no privileged perspective) applied to **anatomy**: "brain" is a human-centric privileging of one dense aggregation, not a different *kind* of thing.

## Proof from the corpus (already attested — not a new dive)
Learning is **not a brain-privilege**; it is an SNN-tissue property at *any* aggregation:
- **F309** — the **isolated *Aplysia* buccal ganglion** performed **operant conditioning** *in a dish*. A ganglion *alone* — a small SNN — does Tier-2 (intent) learning.
- **F309 floor** — even a **single neuron** (B51) does the operant reward-learning analog. So the tissue is complete-enough for Tier-2 down to **one node + its couplings**.
- **F310** — the box jellyfish **rhopalium** (~1000 neurons, ganglion-like) learns **associatively with no central brain at all**.
So the brain adds *scale and density*, not a new learning *capability* — the capability is in the synaptic-neural tissue itself, at whatever aggregation.

## Honest nuance — scale is not ONLY size
A ganglion **is** a small SNN (same substrate, same kind of thing) — but aggregation is not purely additive: denser tissue grows new **organizational topology** (cortical layers, columns, ganglionic specialization, long-range tracts). So "*just* smaller" undersells that scale brings **higher-order organization** on top of the same tissue. The honest statement: **same KIND of structure (synaptic-neural network) at all scales; added organization at density.** A brain is a ganglion's structure *plus* emergent organization — not a different substrate.

## Why it matters
This closes the F311 naming thread structurally: if neural tissue is scale-free, then the name should describe the **tissue/structure** (synaptic-neural network / the stored-relationship coupling), **not** a privileged aggregation ("brain") or a privileged signaling subset ("spiking"). The framework reads net / ganglion / brain as **one scale-free synaptic-neural tissue**, itself the Tier-2 layer riding on the aneural Tier-1 substrate (F308/F310). The "brain" loses its special status the same way the "neuron" did (F311: the node is just where relationships anchor) — privilege dissolves at every level into *relationship + substrate*.
