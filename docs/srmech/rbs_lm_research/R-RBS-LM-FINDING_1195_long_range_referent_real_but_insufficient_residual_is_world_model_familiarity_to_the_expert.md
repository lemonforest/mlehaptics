## Reading — the signal is real, the binding fails, and WHY is the finding

**The referent signal is genuinely real.** P(the | given) = 0.82 vs P(the | new) = 0.65 — a head noun that was mentioned earlier is markedly more likely to take a definite article. Prior mention (the operand's exact recurrence, F1193/F1177) does carry definiteness information. My going-in "the referent binds" hypothesis is **partially supported** at the signal level.

**But it does NOT bind the prediction.** BIGRAM+REFERENT 0.505 vs BIGRAM 0.507 (−0.001) — the same null as F1194's local content (+0.001). Adding the long-range referent to the order model changes nothing. The reason is decisive: **even NEW (first-mention) nouns take "the" 65% of the time.** Narrative fiction is written with a familiar-world stance — "the room", "the door", "the old man" appear definite on first textual mention because the narrator treats the world as already shared. So "the" dominates *both* given (0.82) and new (0.65); the bigram's always-predict-"the" is already near-optimal, and splitting by given/new can't beat it (the referent RULE alone, 0.363, is worse — it wrongly predicts "a" for new nouns that mostly take "the").

**So the residual is one level DEEPER than the text.** Peeling the layers: the disambiguation is not in more ORDER (F1193), not in LOCAL CONTENT (F1194), and not fully in the long-range PRIOR MENTION (F1195, real but insufficient) — it is in the narrator's **familiarity / world model** (which entities are uniquely identifiable in the shared world), which is *above the text entirely*. That is the genuine residual-of-the-residual, and it is exactly the F282 / F552 epistemic ceiling: we found a **real binding signal** but it does not close, because the deciding information is world-model-bound, not text-bound. This is diagnostic, not predictive — we can now name EXACTLY where the residual lives (definiteness-by-familiarity/uniqueness) and hand *that precise question* to the expert (the linguist / the author).

**The k=3 binder is necessary-but-not-sufficient.** The long-range recurrence (prior mention) is a genuine EC signal (F1177/F1188), but the full operand is the referent's status in a **presupposed world model**, only partially encoded in the text. So op(x)operand's binder is not merely textual recurrence — it is the world-model the text points at, which is why the operand ultimately routes to the expert (F282): the peel-back sequence order → content → prior-mention → world-model is the cone of ignorance drawn on grammar, each layer handing a sharper question upward.
