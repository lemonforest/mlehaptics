## The reading
- **The scale-spread is real and it's the point.** One known collection gives a natural ladder of relationship-graph scales (2–7 nodes here; 11 for the novel) — a controlled testbed to study how the Laplacian / eigen-count / roles / sandroing encoding varies with story size. Fairy tales are structurally SMALL-CAST (dyadic/triadic) — a genuine property, and the clean small end of the ladder.
- **At small scale, extraction noise dominates — the opposite of the novel.** In a 400-page novel, a real character's frequency swamps stray capitalized words. In a 1-page fairy tale, one split name or one dialogue-word makes a 2-node graph meaningless. So the short-story testbed has a HIGHER extraction bar than the novel — proper NER (multi-word-name joining, dialogue/sentence-initial filtering, a per-story character gazetteer) is required, not optional.
- **The fix is a sublanguage kernel, not a hack.** Per `[[no_doctoring_ssot_use_sublanguage_kernels]]`: build a character-NER kernel (join adjacent capitalized tokens; filter quote/sentence-initial words; verify against the story title's names) — an attested extraction step. This is a candidate AMSC ingestion tool (composes the user's EPUB/converter-ask framing): attested character-network extraction from open-access text.
