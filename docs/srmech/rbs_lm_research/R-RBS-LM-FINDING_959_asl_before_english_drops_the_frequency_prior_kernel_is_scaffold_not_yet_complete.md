## Why "ASL before English" is the cleanest fix
The frequency prior (the function-word mass) can be handled three ways; ASL is the most structural:
| handling | what it does | finding |
|---|---|---|
| **down-weight** | IDF-weight the function words after they dominate | §80 (upstream patch) |
| **absorb** | bind function words into distinctive phrases (`"the fourth month"`) | F958 |
| **drop** | ASL gloss has *no* articles/copula — the function words **aren't there** | **F959 (this)** |
ASL **drops at the source**: the words that saturated the within-tome prior (F946) and confounded the cross-tome routing (F956/F957) simply don't appear in the gloss. And per **F609** this isn't speculative — meaning-class-explicit ASL already *out-selected* English. ASL gloss ≈ the **content substrate** (function-word-free, topic-comment, spatial — closer to the relationship structure); English ≈ the **function-word-heavy linear projection**. "ASL before English" = work in the content-substrate, project to the English surface last — the MFO substrate/projection split, and **accessibility-native** (ASL is a living language of the Deaf community; the project's ADA foundation).

## Completeness — the honest answer to "if complete enough"
**Not yet, for full translation.** `asl_sign_kernel.toml` is the **structural scaffold** (F608): a sign = a parameter-**chord** (Stokoe handshape/location/movement/orientation/non-manual) with the **sense as the determinative** (σ_B primary), plugged into the F607 sub-language router. `rbs_lm_asl.py` handles SignWriting codepoints / modality. What it has: the **sign *form*** and the structural reading. What it lacks for ASL-before-English: a **complete English→ASL-gloss *translation*** layer — the gloss lexicon at scale + topic-comment reordering + the function-word drop as a real rule (not the crude stoplist proxy used here). And per **F282 the *surface* is the Deaf / sign-linguistics expert's call** — we supply the **structure**, they choose the signs (verify with a Deaf source, MPM).
