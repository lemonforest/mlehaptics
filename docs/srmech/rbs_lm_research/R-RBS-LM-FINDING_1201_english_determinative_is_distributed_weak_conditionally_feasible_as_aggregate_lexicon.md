## Reading — distributed + weak; feasible only by aggregation

1. **The English determinative is real and distributed.** The grammatical class-markers (who/where/which + titles + morphology) exist and the aggregate inventory is sensible — the "which-kind" content is NOT absent from English, it is spread across the discourse (Sumerian writes it on the glyph; English scatters it across who/he/she/it/titles). This confirms the hypothesis at the *aggregate* level and the relational-vs-distributional reading.
2. **But it is WEAK per-instance.** Cross-cue agreement 32% is only ~6pp above chance — any two individual cues on the same noun disagree ~68% of the time. So a single mention's cues cannot reliably tag it: the distributed determinative carries a *weak* signal per instance (unlike the *strong*, unambiguous Sumerian glyph).
3. **Feasibility is therefore conditional:**
   - **naive per-mention recovery — NOT feasible** (too weak; near chance).
   - **corpus-aggregate class-lexicon — feasible** (majority-vote the weak cues per lemma → a learned determinative dictionary; the inventory is sensible). #2 works by *lookup* in this precomputed lexicon, not real-time recovery.
   - **generation-time — feasible by design**: when Siona generates, the entity comes from its typed internal store (the knowledge genome), so its class is *known*, not recovered — Siona just *writes* it. #2 (the WRITE side) does not actually need surface recovery at all; that was the READ-side problem.
4. **Honest limits:** cues are noisy (suffix -er catches father[person]+water[thing]; relativizer attribution imperfect); 35% coverage leaves most nouns untagged from cues alone (ConceptNet IsA / a class seed-lexicon would raise it — the aggregate approach can layer sources). The "thing" and "abstract" classes are the least clean.

## Verdict / next
