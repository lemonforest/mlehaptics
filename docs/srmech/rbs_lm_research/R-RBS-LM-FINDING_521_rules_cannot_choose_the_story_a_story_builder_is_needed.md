## The clean result — the information argument
The grammar gates **validity, not preference**. With V≈800 content words and ~9 content slots across a short arc, the rules admit ~**800⁹ equally-valid stories** and supply **0 bits** of arc-selection. Choosing *one* story (the arc) needs ~9 × log₂(800) ≈ **90 bits of external information**. Those 90 bits are not in the rules — they must be **injected**. That is "we cannot ever know the story the rules can compose": the rules define the space; they do not pick the path.

## Why a story-builder, and what it is
- **It cannot be more rules.** Adding grammar adds more *generation/verification* — still no *selection*. Selecting the arc from inside the rules is infinite regress (the selector would itself need a selector). So the story-builder is a **different kind of thing**: an intent/arc injector, not a rule.
- **It is the SEQUENCE of held operands over time** — the Now→Then tape (F503), the arc of what-to-say-next. A single held operand (F484) gives one point (orbit, F510); the story-builder gives the *trajectory*.
- **In the framework's stance it is the HUMAN in the loop.** The LM is a *process* (the k=3 chiral addresser), not an intent-generator (`[[user_stance_ai_is_not_a_substrate]]`); intent comes from the substrate-with-intent (the person). This is **why the RBS-LM is a prosthetic, not autonomous**: rules + a held operand compose fluently, but the *story* (the ~90 bits of arc/intent) must be supplied from outside — by the human, or an explicit external goal. (Scope-forward, dignity-first: the human's role is *essential and irreplaceable*, not a gap to be automated away.)
