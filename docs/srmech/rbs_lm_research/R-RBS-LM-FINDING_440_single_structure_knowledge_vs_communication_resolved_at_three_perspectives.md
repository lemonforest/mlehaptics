## "Upon more perspectives" — the load-bearing part (measured)
The split is *present* in one coupled object, but **separating a knowledge-discrepancy from a communication-discrepancy is error-correcting only with three perspectives** (the F291 k=3 triality):

| | knowledge error (one perspective holds a different *sector*) | communication error (one perspective flips *chirality*, same content) |
|---|---|---|
| **k=2** (two perspectives) | DISAGREE on sector — but **no majority → can't say who's right** (detect-only parity) | DISAGREE on sign — **can't say who's right** |
| **k=3** (three perspectives) | majority corrects → odd one = **KNOWLEDGE error** (sector/magnitude) | majority corrects → odd one = **COMMUNICATION error** (chirality) |

**The third perspective is exactly what turns "they differ" into "they *know* different things" vs "they're *miscommunicating* the same thing."** Two can detect (and even see *which component* differs); only three can **correct and classify**. This is F291 (k=2 detects, k=3 corrects) applied to the knowledge/communication distinction — and it is *why* "more perspectives" was the right instinct: the single structure becomes error-correctingly separable at the triality.
