## Verdict
**Idioms are to syntax what "went" is to verbs.** The compositional seen generator builds a phrase's meaning from its parts; on an idiom it fails (yields the literal, wrong meaning), so the whole phrase is stored once as a content-addressed seen-exception tome (phrase → meaning). The generator handles regular phrases; idioms are looked up — exactly the F629/F631 shape (a seen generator + a small seen-exception dict), one scale up.

**The seen-rule engine is now symmetric:** morphology AND syntax each = a seen generator (rotate / compositional walk) + a small seen-exception dictionary (irregular words / idioms). Nothing is trained — the exceptions are seen and stored once (a few thousand idioms = the F630 tail, not a giant corpus). And idioms behave like every foundational tome: per-language (F398), content-addressed + attestable (MPM), and discoverable-when-wrong (F625; held F394; confirmed by the expert F282). So a data-center LLM's hardest case — non-compositional meaning — is here just the syntactic entry in the same small seen-exception store. Held open (F394).
