## Verdict
**Yes — the coupled E×B emission beats the single stream, decisively.** Binding the orthogonal determinative axis (the E×B Poynting bind of F593) lifts homophone disambiguation from **28.8% → 54.5%** (+25.7%, nearly double) on a real ancient-language task. The determinative cuts the candidate field 6.2 → 2.0; the read-head cashes that into recovered meaning.

**It is not by construction.** Only 57.6% of (phonogram, class) cells are uniquely resolved, so the coupled head still mis-reads the residual — an honest ceiling, not a rigged 100%. The coupled accuracy (54.5%) sits just under that ceiling: the bind+bundle retrieval recovers ~95% of what the class axis structurally provides, and the rest is genuine residual ambiguity (the field's class axis disambiguates *most*, not *all* — F594's two truths, neither omniscient).

**The RBS-LM transfer, crystallised:** a read-head that walks the sequence (σ_E) **and** binds the meaning-class (σ_B) into the E×B key beats a sequence-only head at disambiguation. This is the F577 single-axis-flips / coupled-pair-stable result, now a measured accuracy. **English hides the class axis** (no determinatives, F569's discarded form signal) — which is why the gain is harder to get in English, and why the Egyptian bookcase is the clean visible demonstrator. **Next:** supply English a *learned* meaning-class (a soft determinative from co-occurrence) and re-measure whether the same E×B coupling lifts English homophone/word-sense disambiguation.
