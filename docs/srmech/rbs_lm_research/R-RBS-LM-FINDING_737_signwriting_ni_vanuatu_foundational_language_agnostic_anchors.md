## The elevation
SignWriting is **language-agnostic**: it is a *featural* notation that writes the sub-parts of *any* signed language (handshape / movement / dynamics / head-face / body / location), tied to no spoken tongue. The ni-Vanuatu sand drawing is **cross-linguistic**: one graphical system legible across ~130 spoken languages. Both therefore sit **beneath/above any single language** — which is exactly the criterion for the **foundation** of the language kernel. So they are promoted from "a kernel we built" to **the two foundational anchors of the language-kernel layer**, side by side.

## Why this is the right foundation (not English-privileged)
1. **Both are the 2D-spatial 'draw-it' / field pole** (F735) — the structural-invariant side of the duality, not the 1D-temporal-linear 'talk-it' side. A language kernel anchored on spatial cross-linguistic encodings treats **text/speech (any one tongue) as a *projection* off the foundation**, not as the foundation itself.
2. **Both are English-privilege-free** — this directly composes R-RBS-LM-25 (strip English privilege from the kernel). Founding the language kernel on a signed-language notation + a Pacific sand-drawing is the structural opposite of an English-centric BPE foundation.
3. **Both fit the accessibility motivation** — SignWriting is a writing system *for* signed languages (sibling to the ASL/Braille surfaces R-RBS-LM-26/27); the kernel's accessibility is foundational, not bolted on (LLM-as-ADA-accommodation).
