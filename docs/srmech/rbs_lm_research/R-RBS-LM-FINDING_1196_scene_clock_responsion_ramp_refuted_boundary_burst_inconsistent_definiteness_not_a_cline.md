## Reading — three honest verdicts, in order of confidence

1. **The within-scene definiteness RAMP is refuted (robust, 6/6).** Fable's pre-registered signature — P(the) rises with τ and resets at breaks — does not exist: r(τ, is-"the") ≈ 0 and within the break-permutation null in every novel. Definiteness does not accumulate as a scene fills. The specific scene-clock oscillator, in the ramp form, is dead.

2. **Definiteness is STATIONARY — NOT a frequency/drift cline (robust, 6/6).** Global-position drift r(pos, is-"the") ≈ 0 everywhere. So for definiteness specifically, fable's frequency-artifact worry is answered on the *other* side: it is neither a phase-ramp nor an amplitude cline — it is a stationary base rate (~0.6–0.7) with, at most, a boundary perturbation. That kills the "definiteness is a Zipf continuum" reading, but it does NOT install the responsion in its place.

3. **The scene-OPENING accommodation burst is real but inconsistent (3/6; replication 1/3).** The one live signal is the *inverted* phase fable did not predict: scene openings (τ<50) are definite-heavy — the narrator writes the scene frame as given ("The room…", "The inn…") at the boundary and it decays. It was **found post-hoc** (after the ramp failed), and the pre-registered replication confirmed only 1/3, so it is suggestive, not established. A tentative (post-hoc, n=6) split: the burst appears in place-anchored scene-*painting* narration (Dickens, Austen's social scenes, Treasure Island's inns/island) and is absent in interior/reflective (Frankenstein, Jane Eyre) and novelty-cataloguing (Swift) narration — but that is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Honest caveats
- **Post-hoc + multiplicity:** the opening-bump was noticed in the data, and across 6 novels × 2 statistics a corrected threshold (~0.008) leaves only Treasure Island (p=.002) surviving. Treat the 3/6 as exploratory.
- **Segmentation power:** scene boundaries were detected crudely (chapter markers + temporal shifters). Noisy segmentation dilutes the true-break signal, so the 3 nulls may be under-powered rather than truly flat — the mixed result may reflect segmentation quality as much as a genre split. A cleaner segmenter would re-power the boundary test.
- **Scale:** the ramp/boundary test is the *scene* scale. Fable's *entity-level* fractal follow-up — the referring-NP length contracting to a fixed point per entity ("a large old house" → "the house" → "it"), the attractor-contraction taken literally — is untested and is the cleaner intrinsic-responsion probe; this null does not speak to it.
