## The reading (the honest answer to the question)
- **The DSL already tells Siona a lot** — how ops chain into pipelines, how classes compose, and (rc117) the carrier ladder's rungs and promote/project ops. Siona is NOT reinventing those; it reads them. The register-as-conversion-ladder (F1039/F1040) is Siona reading `carrier_ladder_descriptor()`, not hardcoding the ladder.
- **The one thing it can't tell Siona is the per-op rung**, for a precise reason: the QM/Hurwitz op-family is hand-coded Python by design (CLAUDE.md: "the srmech.qm.* physics op-families STAY Python"), so those ops have no DSL descriptor; and even in the tool_schema the operand dimension lives in the SUMMARY prose ("8-vector"), not a structured field. So "octonion consumes dim-8" is not machine-readable, and Siona bridges it with the algebra-name map (`octonion→8`) — which is honest (the name IS the rung) but IS the hardcode the question is pointing at.
- **The fix makes the DSL the SSoT for routing too:** a structured per-op carrier contract (§87) — the param declares its `carrier`+`rung`, or the descriptor carries an ops→{consumes/produces rung} map — and Siona reads the target rung from the declaration instead of the name. Then the answer to "why doesn't the DSL tell Siona" becomes "it does," across chaining, composition, AND carrier routing. Until then, the name-map is the honest bridge, and it is one small, declared closed class (R/C/H/O/S), not open-ended guessing.
