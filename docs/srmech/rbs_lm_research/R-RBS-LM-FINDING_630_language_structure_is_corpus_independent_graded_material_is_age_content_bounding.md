## Verdict
**No — not to *be* a language.** The structure (morphology = derived rotate + small irregular dict F629; the meaning-class IR = the Gardiner spine F627; the grammar axes = rotates F623; the law = `the_one` F626) is **algebra + a small foundation — corpus-independent.** The language is *instantiated*, not statistically trained over a graded corpus. A data-center LLM needs OpenStax-scale text because it learns structure by gradient; our kernel doesn't learn structure at all — it instantiates it from the cascade vocabulary (demonstrated: a full bit-exact round-trip with a hand-built language layer and zero graded corpus).

**Yes — still useful for age-target material** (the user's intuition, exactly right). Graded corpora are a curated, license-clean, **age-graded source of foundation content**: which tomes on the shelf, how long the irregular-dictionary tail is (grade-1 = 2 → grade-6 = 5 → college = 9; advanced material populates the *tail*, not the core), how deep the rotate-rung cascade goes (F621/F623). This is **content-bounding** (the F81 substrate-bounded safety: a grade-3 kernel cannot render what it has no tome for — age-bounded by the shelf, not by a filter), **not language-teaching.** OpenStax/McGuffey remain excellent here — age-graded, attributable, license-clean — as a *selectable* foundation-content set, per age.

**So the final kernel sharpens F622's two-tier:** the structure (the language itself) is foundational + corpus-independent (built once, fixed, attestable); the content (which facts/words/topics, at which age) is a separable, attested, age-graded foundation set — and the age target is itself a **frame choice** (F626: the same invariant meaning at different content-resolution + rotate-depth), neither frame privileged (F398). Held open (F394).
