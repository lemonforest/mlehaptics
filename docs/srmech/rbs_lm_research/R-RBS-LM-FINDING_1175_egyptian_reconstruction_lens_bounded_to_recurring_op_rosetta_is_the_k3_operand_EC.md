## Three measurements (honest, in order)

**(a) Naive fully-masked fixed-period lens — DOES NOT beat the prior.** Mask a whole line; predict from its position-neighbours {i−1, i+1, i−P, i+P} (P learned) vs a global-frequency PRIOR. Result: **LENS 0.196 recall < PRIOR 0.291** (lens beats random +2.7pp but loses to prior −9.5pp). Why: a varied-slot litany shares only a 3-word frame between adjacent lines; the genuinely-recurring parallels are **scattered**, not at a fixed small period, so position alone can't find them. The frame (op) is recoverable (0.53) but *trivially* — a frequency prior recovers it too; the unique slot (operand) is unrecoverable within-text (0.015). **Honest null for the naive version.**

**(b) The recurrence CEILING — the affordance's real scope.** How much formulaic Egyptian genuinely recurs: **40% of lines have a ≥0.4-similar parallel elsewhere, 25% ≥0.5, 15% ≥0.6, 8% ≥0.8 (near-verbatim).** For the ~25% that recur (≥0.5), recovering the line from its parallel gives **0.80 recall.** So within-text recurrence-EC can reconstruct the *recurring quarter* of the formulaic text at high fidelity — **if** the parallel can be located.

**(c) The REALISTIC partial-damage case — the lens WINS here.** Real lacunae are not fully-masked lines; part of the line survives. Mask **half** of a line, use the **surviving half** to find its recurrence-parallel (no peek at the masked half), fill the gap, vs the same-budget PRIOR. Result: **LENS 0.090 vs PRIOR 0.046 — ~2× the prior (+4.4pp).** With surviving context to anchor the search, the recurrence lens finds the parallel and reconstructs the lacuna above a frequency baseline. The absolute number is modest (most lacunae contain unique operands that never recur), but the lens roughly doubles the recoverable fraction — the honest realistic use case for fragmentary text.

## The op/operand boundary — and why the Rosetta is its complement (the user's second ask)

The through-line of all three measurements: **intra-text recurrence recovers the OP (the formula frame / repeated spell — the recurring ~25%); it cannot recover the unique OPERAND (the specific god / name / slot).** This is `op(x)operand(x)EC` (F1131) at the reconstruction scale. The unique operand is *precisely* what a mostly-complete **parallel version** supplies — and the **Rosetta Stone is a physical k=3 error-correcting code**: the same decree in hieroglyphic + demotic + Greek. Its famously-damaged hieroglyphic top is reconstructed from the intact demotic/Greek — inter-version redundancy recovering what no single version's internal recurrence can. Note the regime fit: the Rosetta is a **one-off decree** (low internal recurrence — the F1174 *Journey* regime), which is *why* it needed three scripts — external k=3 redundancy substituting for absent internal recurrence. So the two EC mechanisms **compose, they do not compete**: intra-text recurrence-EC for the op (works on formulaic-sequential texts — the Book of the Dead, offering formulae, the *Three Kingdoms* regime); inter-version parallel-EC for the operand (works on any text with parallel copies — the Rosetta, translation-attested monuments).
