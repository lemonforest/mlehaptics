## The unanimous verdict (3-of-3)
**Biological synaptic plasticity is a CONDUCTANCE / receptor / structural change — NOT a capacitance change:**
- **What changes (LTP/LTD):** postsynaptic **AMPA-receptor number** (trafficking — exo/endocytosis, primary) + **single-channel conductance** (GluA1 Ser831 phosphorylation; PKA) + **dendritic-spine structure** (head enlargement/shrinkage). Gated by **NMDA-receptor Ca²⁺ coincidence detection → CaMKII (LTP) vs calcineurin (LTD)**.
- **Circuit terms:** a "stronger synapse" = larger **g_syn (conductance)**; membrane **Cₘ ≈ 0.9–1.0 µF/cm² is treated as a near-constant** and does NOT store the learned weight.
- **STDP:** NMDA Ca²⁺ timing/magnitude → CaMKII/calcineurin; **no capacitance involvement.**
- **Memcapacitance:** **NO attested role in biological learning.** Every "LTP in a membrane" result is in **artificial droplet-interface-bilayer (DIB) systems** for neuromorphic computing (Najem/Sarles, ORNL/UT/Vanderbilt; PNAS 2022 doi:10.1073/pnas.2212195119; PNAS 2025 doi:10.1073/pnas.2510664122; ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces) — explicitly biomimetic *models*, NOT living neurons. **All three tiers found + flagged this independently.**
- **Falsification:** "synaptic plasticity = the membrane capacitor's memory" is **NOT supported** for biological synapses.

## Honest residue (opus tier sharpest)
The negative is "absence of *established* role," **NOT "proven absent"** — whether membrane capacitance plays *any* in-vivo role in real neurons is genuinely **open** (unattested, not refuted).
