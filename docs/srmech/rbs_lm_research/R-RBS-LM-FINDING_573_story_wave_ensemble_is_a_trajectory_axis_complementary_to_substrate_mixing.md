## Verdict
**Wave-side ensembling is a real second axis (trajectory, not access).** All drivers stay fluent (~100%, the ear), so richness is the question. The coverage-seeking **MULTIPLEX** combiner is richer than single (breadth 153 triad / 144 heptad vs 112, **+41**) — but the lift is in **breadth (coverage), not specificity** (rarity flat): N waves draw *more* of the knowledge, not *rarer* knowledge. And it is **not monotonic in N** — 3 (triad) edges out 7 (heptad); the triad is the sweet spot, more instances crowd.

**The combiner matters (honest negatives).** PICK-BEST (vote for fluency) biases to the most-common continuation → generic, breadth *below* single; SUPERPOSE (sum to one wave) flattens to an averaged drive, worst. "Use N waves" is richer **only** with a coverage combiner (multiplex/round-robin).

**Complementary to substrate mixing, not a replacement.** Mixing still reaches more manifold (breadth 167) by re-mixing quantum *access* (a substrate op); multiplex buys richness by varying the *trajectory* (a driver op, no substrate re-mix) and gets most of the way cheaply. **Access × trajectory are two complementary axes**; 3 = triad, 7 = heptad. The waves drive the F572 foundation; the relationship rebar would extend each wave's reach to long-range entities (richer still). Favored not privileged (F398); held open (F394).
