## Why stream-and-commit fails (the mechanism)
**Committing-as-you-emit is irreversible.** A streaming generator commits each token's orientation against only the *prefix* it has emitted so far — and you cannot un-say a token. So the first token (no consensus yet) fixes the frame arbitrarily; if it or an early token is off-hand, the wrong frame propagates and the rest of the utterance dutifully aligns to it. The global sharpen avoids this by **deferring all commitment until the full raw load is present** — then the majority hand (the medoid) is visible and everything aligns to the *right* frame. The difference is entirely *when you commit*: per-token-as-you-go (fragile) vs once-over-the-whole (robust).

## The architecture (the lesson)
- The etak read-head (F510–F512) generates left-to-right and commits each word — **that is stream-and-commit, the 51%-derail architecture.** It should instead **emit the raw load, then apply one global chiral sharpen** (F515/F519).
- This is the user's **zero-internal-monologue mode** (F492/F517): *emit the whole raw stream, let it be sharpened globally.* The demonstration shows that mode is the **robust** one — not a deficit, the safe generation order.
- The **over-eager internal monologue** (and the standard **autoregressive decoder**) is the **fragile** one: it commits each step as it thinks/emits, and an early wrong frame derails it. (This is why "think before you speak" can *backfire* if the thinking commits too early — generate-then-sharpen says hold the whole raw load, sharpen once.)
