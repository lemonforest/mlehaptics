## The reading (stop-and-think = assemble more cues)
- **Automatic recall = one strong cue (`k=1`).** A recent or strong memory sits well above the floor; a single cue retrieves it instantly, no effort. (Margin high, `k=1` suffices.)
- **"Stop and think" = the deliberate assembly of multiple cues (`k>1`).** A faded/distant memory has receded toward the floor (F975/F976: the one-rung memoryless recession — forgetting). One cue no longer reaches it (N=500, single LOST). So you **stop and gather more retrieval cues** — where, when, who else, what came before/after — and their **superposition** gives the √k altitude lift (F976) that raises the faded trace back above the floor (multi-cue FOUND). *That assembling is the effort; the √k is the payoff.* This is the ordinary cued-recall / multiple-retrieval-cue / tip-of-the-tongue structure of memory (established cognitive science — no lineage; verify with a memory researcher, MPM).
- **The forgetting rate is invariant (F976 — the honest part).** Stop-and-think does **not** un-forget: the memory still decayed at the one-rung `|q|` rate (the ground truth of loss). What multi-cue does is **raise the altitude** (start √k higher), recovering a trace that *had* fallen below the single-cue floor — not reverse the decay. You retrieve *more of what remains*, from more angles; you do not restore what the rung-rate already erased. "What is not preserved is lost to time" holds; "stop and think" reaches lower toward the floor by superposing perspectives, but cannot go below what the rate has destroyed.
- **Why this fits the project (accessibility/BCI).** It gives a *structural* account of recall effort: the "stop and think" cost is the cost of assembling `k` cues, and the payoff is √k margin — a measurable, substrate-native picture of why distant memories take deliberate effort while recent ones are automatic (`[[feedback_llm_as_ada_accommodation_bci_proves_it]]`; `[[user_stance_llm_is_human_knowledge_responding_to_1d_t_asymptotic]]`).
