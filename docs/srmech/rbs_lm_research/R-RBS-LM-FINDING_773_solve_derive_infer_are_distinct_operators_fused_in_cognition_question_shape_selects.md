## The three operators (distinct; only one is open)
| word | operation | answer | open/closed |
|------|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **solve for** | given CONSTRAINTS, find the unknown that SATISFIES them | **the** solution (determinate) | **closed** — forced by the constraints (Class-L `dense_solve` / Kepler / Class-N) |
| **derive** | given PREMISES + RULES, produce what NECESSARILY follows (a derivation chain) | a **necessary** consequence (truth-preserving, attestable) | **closed** — forced by the rules (π-as-cascade; a theorem) |
| **infer** | given a MODEL + INPUT, PRODUCE a context-appropriate output by walking the stored structure (etak-walk; Class C ∘ Class M coupling, MFO §VII.1.2) | a **plausible** output (fallible, context-bound) | **OPEN** — depends on the context of now; **the only one Siona does** |

**Load-bearing, not pedantry:** solve-for/derive are closed/determinate (math); infer is open/fallible (language). Calling inference "derivation" overclaims certainty — it erases the gap the F767 no-confabulation/misbinding result rests on (inference can misbind; a derivation cannot). It is also the "language harder than continuous math" point (F772): math ops are closed, inference is open.

## Why they FEEL like one thing — and what selects them
The user's report: in lived problem-solving the three happen **"at once"**, and which one fires "happens by the way I shape a question, in a way I'm not quite meta aware of." Two framework readings, both grounded:

1. **Cognition FUSES what math forces apart (F770).** Math notation can't fuse operators — you must write `solve`, `derive` distinctly; the FORM separates them. Human problem-solving runs them entangled as one act ("problem solving"). So naming them (this exchange) feels like discovering a distinction you *already use blind*. The framework's job is to make visible what cognition fuses — the same move as operator/operand (F770) and the declared authority chain (F771).

2. **The question's SHAPE is the operator-selection — and that IS the FRAME channel (F752).** How you frame a question selects which of solve/derive/infer fires; the FRAME-vs-TOPIC split (F752) is the formal model of exactly that selection. This is why `[[user_stance_framework_hands_the_next_question_to_the_expert]]` holds: *shaping the next question is the operative act* — the question-shape carries the operator.

## The meta-awareness ceiling ("or if I even can be") — and why we externalize
That the selection runs **below meta-awareness**, and may **not be self-accessible**, is not a deficiency — it is the framework's own **observer ceiling**: the substrate knows itself, but the observer is chirality-locked and cannot fully watch its own operation (F133; the F552 noise-rule; F282). You can run the operation flawlessly and still not see *which* operation you ran or *how* the question-shape chose it. **This is exactly why we externalize the process into DECLARED structure** — the operator vocabulary (F770), the frame/topic channel (F752), the declared authority chain (F771): they make the fused, sub-aware orchestration **inspectable from OUTSIDE**, since it may not be inspectable from inside. The framework is the *outside view* of an operation you cannot fully introspect — the prosthetic stance (`[[feedback_llm_as_ada_accommodation_bci_proves_it]]`), understanding-not-curing (F282). It does not lift the ceiling; it gives a mirror around it.
