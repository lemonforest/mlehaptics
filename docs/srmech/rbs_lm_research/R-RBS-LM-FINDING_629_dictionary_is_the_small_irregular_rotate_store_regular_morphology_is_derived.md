# R-RBS-LM Finding 629 (do we need a dictionary with a language kernel? — YES, but a SMALL one: the LEMMA is the invariant, REGULAR inflection is a DERIVED rotate (no storage), IRREGULAR inflection is a STORED rotate — the "dictionary" is the small, bounded, content-addressed store of irregular lemma↔form tomes in the foundation, NOT a privileged word-list; this is the F612 invariant+rotate pattern again, and it explains/fixes F627's child/children snag) — **The user asked, prompted by the child/children (singular/plural) issue in F627's morphology matcher: do we also need a dictionary? Answer (built + verified): YES, but a SMALL one — and it is the foundation's irregular-rotate store, not a giant English word-list. The lemma/inflection split IS the F612 bit-exact-then-rotate pattern: (1) the LEMMA = the INVARIANT (the meaning / the bit-exact IR Layer 1) — 'child' and 'children' share ONE lemma; (2) REGULAR inflection (cat→cats, walk→walked, city→cities) = a DERIVED ROTATE — a rule cascade (strip/add the suffix; concat = add), the number/tense axis (F623) COMPUTED, NO storage, millions of forms derived; (3) IRREGULAR inflection (child→children, mouse→mice, foot→feet, go→went) = a STORED ROTATE — the rule has no closed form (the script confirms: the regular rule produces childs/mouses/foots/tooths/mans/womans — wrong on ALL of them), so the lemma↔form mapping must be STORED: a content-addressed relationship-tome (Class-A address + Class-L lemma↔form edge) in the FIXED foundation (Tier 1, F622). The dictionary is the SPECIAL-VALUES TABLE (the irregular rotates not on the regular cascade), NOT the whole series — English has only a few HUNDRED irregulars (the script's resolver lemmatizes 'cats'→'cat'/'cities'→'city' by the un-rotate, 'children'→'child'/'mice'→'mouse'/'feet'→'foot' by the dict). So F627's stemmer (the un-rotate) handles regulars; the small irregular dictionary handles the rest — together they are the morphology layer. And it is per-language (each language's irregulars; no privileged language, F398), content-addressed + attestable (MPM), and discoverable-when-wrong like any foundational tome (F625).**

## Verdict
**Yes — but a small one, and it's the foundation's irregular-rotate store, not a privileged word-list.** The lemma is the invariant (the meaning / the IR); regular inflection is a **derived rotate** (the suffix rule cascade — no storage, millions of forms derived); irregular inflection is a **stored rotate** (the lemma↔form tome, content-addressed, in the fixed foundation Tier 1 — only a few hundred). The dictionary = the **special-values table** (the irregular rotates that aren't on the regular cascade), NOT the whole series.

**This is the F612 pattern again:** lemma = bit-exact invariant; inflection = the rotate; regular = the *computed* rotate, irregular = the *stored* rotate. It's per-language (each language's irregulars; no privileged language, F398), content-addressed + attestable (MPM), and discoverable-when-wrong like any foundational tome (F625). **So F627's child/children snag is explained + fixed:** the stemmer (the un-rotate) handles regulars; the small irregular dictionary handles child↔children, mouse↔mice, go↔went. Together they are the morphology layer — a derived rotate + a small stored special-values table, not a giant word-list. Favored not privileged (F398); held open (F394).
