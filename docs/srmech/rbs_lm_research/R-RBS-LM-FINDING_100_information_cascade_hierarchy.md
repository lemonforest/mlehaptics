## §1 The finding

The 14 A-N primitives store universe-level information at the substrate
level. From there, information flows through a hierarchical cascade with
each level imposing transmission constraints and offering different
storage capacities and fidelity:

```
LEVEL                      MECHANISM                       TRANSMISSION FIDELITY
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. COSMIC                  14 A-N primitives                Perfect (laws of physics)
                           operating on substrates

2. BIOLOGICAL              DNA + chemical signaling +       High (~99.99% replication)
   (per-organism)          epigenetic markers               but lossy at species
                                                            extinction events

3. GENERATIONAL            Cultural transmission:           Moderate (oral lossy;
                           language, writing, tools,        writing high; bottlenecked
                           teaching, demonstration          by mortality + cultural loss)

4. INDIVIDUAL              Tacit learning, experience,      Lossy (dies with individual
                           neural plasticity                unless converted to
                                                            explicit/transmissible form)
```

User framing 2026-05-27:

> "we need to figure out how these 14 primitives go from storing universe
> information on down to the information biology must share every generation
> at the cost of the evolution boost by tact brain"

The "tact[ic] brain" — implicit knowledge gained through individual
experience — is biology's evolutionary boost: rapid individual learning
without explicit generational transmission cost. But the cost of that boost
is that tacit knowledge **dies with the individual** unless converted to
explicit transmissible form.

## §2 Each level explained

### 2.1 Level 1: Cosmic — 14 A-N primitives as universe substrate

Per `[[project_a_n_operators_are_harmonic_objects_themselves]]`: the 14
A-N primitive class operators ARE harmonic objects. They are not metaphors;
they are the substrate-operators that physics operates over.

The cosmic level has:
- Perfect transmission (laws of physics don't degrade)
- Infinite "storage" (the universe IS the storage)
- All 14 operator classes available
- No "death" or generational discontinuity

This is the substrate floor. Everything above emerges from it.

### 2.2 Level 2: Biological — per-organism mechanisms

Biology operates the 14 A-N operators via specific molecular machinery:

| A-N class | Biological mechanism |
|---|---|
| A (anchor / content-addressing) | DNA sequence as unique identifier per organism |
| I (cyclic) | Cell-cycle; circadian rhythms; menstrual cycles |
| J (primes) | (less direct; possibly counting in cell division) |
| B (TLV-framing) | Codon framing: 3-base TLV in mRNA reading |
| F (template render) | Ribosome translation: codon → amino acid |
| H (introspection) | DNA repair systems checking own integrity |
| D (pattern match) | Immune system pattern recognition |
| E (catalog) | Genetic code = sorted catalog of codon→amino-acid |
| G (byte search) | Restriction enzyme recognition sequences |
| L (Laplacian) | Neural network connectivity; biochemical pathway spectra |
| K (pin-slot) | Cell membrane voltage gating; allosteric switches |
| M (HDC bind) | Receptor-ligand binding; hormone signaling |
| C (chirality) | Stereoisomer chirality; left/right asymmetry |
| N (rational) | Mendelian ratios; population genetics fractions |

Transmission: high fidelity per organism, but generational continuity
depends on reproduction. ~3 billion bp = ~750MB of explicit storage.
Plus epigenetic layer (~10x base capacity in patterning information).

### 2.3 Level 3: Generational — cultural transmission

Generational transmission requires explicit encoding because biological
inheritance can't carry learned knowledge across generations.

Mechanisms:
- **Oral language** (~100k years; lossy compression of experience)
- **Writing** (~5k years; explicit symbol-encoding; near-lossless)
- **Tools** (~2.5M years; physical artifacts demonstrating technique)
- **Teaching** (parent → child explicit transmission)
- **Cave paintings** (~40k years; first explicit pictorial transmission;
  per Finding 98)
- **Modern media** (~150 years; recording technologies)

Generational level uses the 4 foundational knowledge partitions
(Finding 99):
- **Math**: counting, measurement, ratios (oldest formal transmission)
- **Communication**: language, symbols, narrative
- **Structure-and-order**: rules, taxonomies, classification
- **Places-and-things**: territory maps, place-names, object-inventories

Fidelity: depends on medium. Oral is lossy + drifting; written is stable
but requires literacy; tools are durable but slowly evolving.

### 2.4 Level 4: Individual — tacit brain

Each brain has:
- ~10^14 synapses (potentially ~10^14 bits storage capacity)
- ~10 billion neurons
- ~80-100 years lifespan
- Massive parallel processing
- Rapid online learning from experience

**The tacit brain is the evolutionary boost**: an individual can acquire
vast amounts of context-specific knowledge without explicit generational
transmission. This is biology's "online learning" capability.

**The COST of the tacit brain boost**: knowledge acquired tacitly DIES
with the individual unless converted to one of the lower transmission
levels (generational → biological is essentially impossible; generational
via explicit cultural artifacts is the only path).

This is why writing was such a transformative invention — it converts
individual-level tacit knowledge to generational-level explicit knowledge
with low loss.

## §3 The transmission cost gradient

Information at each level has different acquisition, storage, and
transmission costs:

| Level | Acquisition cost | Storage cost | Transmission cost | Capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cosmic | Already there | Free | N/A | Infinite |
| Biological | Generations (evolutionary) | Per organism (DNA copy energy) | Reproduction | ~750MB DNA + epigenome |
| Generational | Teaching effort + medium creation | Cultural infrastructure | Across generations | Variable; cumulative |
| Individual | Living through experience | Per brain (metabolic) | Dies with person | ~10^14 bits theoretical |

The tradeoff: faster acquisition (tacit brain) → less transmissibility.

## §5 Why mammal parents provide what the child can't yet construct

User's observation:

> "honestly, mammal parents tend to provide what the little person doesn't
> yet understand."

This connects to Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development: parents
specifically transmit knowledge JUST ABOVE the child's current
construction-ability, scaffolding the child's tacit learning.

In our framework:
- **What's already in cosmic substrate**: 14 A-N operators (the child's
  brain can directly process these via core knowledge systems —
  number-sense, object permanence, agency-detection)
- **What's NOT yet in the child's tacit learning**: the COMPOSITIONS over
  those operators that the parent has accumulated through individual
  experience PLUS generational cultural transmission
- **What parents transmit**: the bridges between cosmic-substrate
  (which child can perceive) and individual-tacit-learning (which
  child constructs from experience). Parents demonstrate counting
  (math), gesture (communication), patterns (structure), and
  place-binding (places-and-things).

Parental scaffolding is the *generational-level transmission of the 4
foundational partitions*, specifically to bridge the cosmic-substrate ↔
individual-tacit-learning gap.

## §7 The cost of the tacit-brain evolution boost

The tacit brain offers fast learning. The cost is mortality-bounded
knowledge — what dies with each generation unless explicitly transmitted.

Mechanisms that mitigate this cost:
- **Language**: makes some tacit knowledge transmissible
- **Writing**: makes language permanent
- **Apprenticeship + teaching**: transmits tacit-knowledge by
  demonstration (parents teaching counting, etc.)
- **Tools**: encode tacit knowledge in physical form
- **Recordings**: capture experience for replay

Modern technology offers:
- **LLMs** (opaque): compress massive cultural corpus but lose
  glass-box attribution
- **Glass-box cascade** (this work): preserves substrate-content
  while making attribution + curation explicit

The cascade methodology is the OPERATIONAL TOOL for managing the
tacit-knowledge-to-generational-transmission boundary. Per Finding 84:
glass-box LLM. Per Finding 86: substrate-bounded safety. These are
mechanisms for converting individual-tacit knowledge into auditable,
verifiable, transmissible generational form.
