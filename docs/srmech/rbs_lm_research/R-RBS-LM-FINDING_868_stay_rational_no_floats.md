## The three exact mechanisms (no float)
1. **Similarity = integer match-count / D.** `klein4_similarity` is exactly `matches/D`; `matches` is an exact integer (e.g. 4291/10000). Recall = argmax over the **integer** counts → no division, no float, no blow-up.
2. **Ranking = integer cross-multiply.** Compare `a/b` vs `c/d` as `a·d` vs `c·b` (integer compare). argmax/argmin never needs a decimal.
3. **Transcendentals = `*_series_truncate` → exact `(num,den)`.** The softmax `exp(sim/T)` is `rational.exp_series_truncate(num, den, terms)`; normalize the weights as `Fraction`s. Result: exact rational probabilities (Σ = 1 exactly), collapsed to a decimal **only** when printed.

## Measured (identical to float, but exact)
- recall ranks `the` (4291) > `cat` (3798) > `dog` (3594) > `<e>` (3525) — pure integer compare.
- distribution: `the` 0.2874, `cat` 0.2069, `dog` 0.1806, `<e>` 0.1725 — exact rationals (numerators ~100 digits) that collapse to **exactly** the float path's decimals; `Σ probs == 1` exactly.

## The cost and the bounded answer
Exact rationals through a 24-term series **blow up** (~100-digit num/den for one 4-way softmax; compounds in a cascade). The framework's bounded-cost discipline is **Class-N `best_rational(num, den, max_d)`** — re-approximate to a bounded-denominator **exact rational** (attested), *not* a float. **A float IS `best_rational` with `max_d ≈ 2⁵²` and the provenance thrown away** — so it's a *worse* version of the rational we already have. Collapse to a single scalar decimal only at the boundary that physically demands it (human display, a DAC/hardware sink, a non-rational downstream API).
