## The serializer — Klein-4 HV ⇄ 2-bit-packed bytes (the boolean belly)
A Klein-4 HV is D=8192 slots, each a 4-sector value ∈ {0,1,2,3} = exactly **2 bits**. srmech's native `HV.tobytes()` stores 1 byte/slot (8192 B). Packing 4 slots/byte → **2048 B/HV** (the boolean-belly 2-bit packing), and `HV.from_sequence(secs, sectors=4)` reconstructs bit-identically:
- `hv_to_bits(hv)`: `tolist()` → pack `secs[i]` into bit-pair `(i&3)*2` of byte `i>>2`. **2048 B** (was 8192 via native tobytes).
- `bits_to_hv(b)`: unpack the bit-pairs → `HV.from_sequence(secs, sectors=4)`.
- **Round-trip exact: True** (a single random HV verifies before any article work).

## The instrument = the chunked-M, bit-serialized
Per article: `make_chunks(toks)` builds the F879 recall structure — `klein4_bind(key_at(ctx,pos), wv(token))` over the body, `chunk_bundle`'d into C Klein-4 chunk HVs. **That bundle of chunk HVs IS the instrument.** Serialize: write each chunk's 2048 packed bytes to `art{idx}.rbs`. Recall standalone: read the `.rbs` back, `bits_to_hv` each chunk, run the F879 phase-keyed `stream` (chunk_resolve against the codebook) — reproducing the article from the disk bits with **no re-encode**.

## Three honest readings
1. **Serialization is lossless; the instrument is real.** 10/10 bit-identical HVs and 10/10 identical recall (in-memory vs from-disk) prove the `.rbs` artifact carries the full instrument. Serialization adds **zero** error.
2. **The one "miss" is HIDDEN FIBER, not an error (introspected per F552 / [[user_stance_no_information_without_value]]).** I first wrote it off as the F896 capacity wall — wrong, and exactly the dismissive read the noise rule forbids. Introspecting article #8 (`bankside`): the first divergence is at the bigram `('of','southwark')`, which the resolver ranks `<e>` (0.4454) just over the correct `it` (0.4049) — margin **0.04**. Why so close? The article contains the **4-gram `london borough of southwark` verbatim TWICE** — once in body prose (`the london borough of southwark` → `it`) and once in the **`category` markup footer** (`category london borough of southwark` → `<e>`). They are identical context until the token *before* the 4-gram (`the` vs `category`), so the article's de Bruijn unique-walk depth is **k\*=5** — and at K=5 the instrument recalls it **EXACT** (K=2/3/4 all miss; K=5/6 exact). So the recall is NOT lossy at capacity; it is faithfully superposing two genuinely-repeated relationships and resolving them once context reaches the phrase's real k*. **The fiber: the simplewiki `category <pagename>` footer re-states the page's own subject verbatim — a markup/self-reference structure** that surfaces as a recall "miss" precisely where body prose and footer collide. This points straight at the **form-layer kernel (#225/#226, F607)**: a markup-aware encode would tag the `category` footer distinctly from body prose and break the collision at K=2 without needing the longer context. The "error" was the article telling us about its own markup.
3. **The materialized HDC is LARGER than text (~48×), not smaller.** This is the honest, load-bearing result (NO overclaim — the retracted 80-byte "0.00×" was a measurement artifact; this is the genuine size of the materialized chunked-M). A persisted vector blob is the dense-leaning thing. **This is exactly why the sparse persistence layer is the regenerable RECIPE (F897), not the vector blob** — you keep the 210 B recipe + regenerate the 10 KB instrument on demand. The bit-serialized instrument is the right artifact for a **C-host / no-Python-encoder** environment (recall with no re-encode), traded against size.
