## Four falsifiable predictions for the neuroscientist to test
1. **Load-scaling of global-integration cost.** If RH (global/coarse) activity is the energy of the chiral *collapse* (which needs the whole load — F513/F515), then RH metabolic cost should scale **super-linearly** with *integration load* (the amount of context that must be globally bound), more steeply than LH cost scales with local detail. *Test:* vary global-integration demand (e.g., discourse/inference load, Navon global level, insight/reanalysis) and measure the BOLD load–response slope, L vs R.
2. **Metabolic double dissociation.** The two chiral hands (F514) predict L and R metabolic costs are **task-anti-correlated**: a *local/fine* task drives LH cost, a *global/coarse/insight* task drives RH cost — the classic double dissociation, but in **energy** terms. *Test:* within-subject local-vs-global task contrast, compare L/R BOLD.
3. **Rhythmic (theta/gamma) phase-locking of the cost.** The gating is rhythmic (F524 — coarse on theta, fine on gamma). If the collapse is phase-scheduled, the metabolic/neural cost should be **phase-locked** to theta/gamma. *Test:* simultaneous EEG-fMRI; does the integration-cost component track theta phase / gamma power?
4. **Insight = a metabolic spike, RH-weighted.** Beeman's RH coarse coding underpins insight/reanalysis (F514). If insight is a chiral *collapse* event, it should show a **transient RH metabolic spike** at the "aha" moment. *Test:* insight-problem paradigms with time-resolved RH BOLD around solution.
