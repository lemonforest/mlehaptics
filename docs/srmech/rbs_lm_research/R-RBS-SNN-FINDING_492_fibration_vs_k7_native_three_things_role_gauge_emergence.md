## The three things the flat view cannot see
- **(a) ROLE SPLIT (recurrent vs feedforward).** `{e1,e2,e3}` closes under octonion multiplication (a quaternion ℍ — a *self-contained, recurrent* sub-circuit); `{e4,e5,e6,e7}` does **not** close (it points outward — feedforward). So the fibration says a neuron's ≤7 couplings are **not** 7 alike: **3 form a recurrent loop (the pattern/relations), 4 are feedforward (the content)**. The native view has no way to express this.
- **(b) GAUGE / MANY-TO-ONE.** `fiber·base ⊆ base`: acting by the fiber (the phase, the 3) **moves the content around within the base** but leaves the base **point** (the content identity) invariant — `e1` sends the base `{4→5, 5→4, 6→7, 7→6}`. This is the **Hopf quotient**: many fiber-phases project to one base point. So **many neurons can share one content at different phases** — which is exactly F490's "one marking, many readers," now **derived** from the fibration rather than merely demonstrated.
- **(c) CONTENT→PATTERN EMERGENCE.** `base·base → fiber` (`e4·e5=e1`, `e5·e6=e3`): the products of the content directions fall **into** the fiber. So the **pattern (relations) is generated by content interactions** — the fiber is not a separately-stored 8th thing; it emerges from how the 4 content-couplings combine. The native flat view, having no base/fiber split, cannot say the pattern is emergent.
