## §1 The hidden fiber

The framework's foundational object here is the **fiber**: structure that is *real in the total space but absent from the base space until projected down*. This is not a metaphor reached for to fit the occasion — it is the project's core encoding stance ("fiber as spatially-absent encoding"), and it has a prior reading on record: **F229** read *temperature* as a fiber — a control parameter that is real but absent from the token stream until it is projected into the act of sampling.

An **unseen disability is a fiber in exactly this sense:**

| | base space (what the observer reads) | fiber (what is real but absent) |
|---|---|---|
| **seen disability** | the physical fact is *in* the base space — it self-projects | (small residual fiber) |
| **unseen disability** | nothing — "can't be seen" | the entire disability lives here, real and load-bearing, **spatially absent** until projected |

The disability is not less real for being absent from the base space — a fiber is fully real in the total space. It is *unprojected*. The whole difficulty, and the whole exclusion, follows from one fact: **bringing a fiber into the base space costs something.**

## §2 The mechanism — why exclusion is the *default*, not a choice

Three framework pieces compose into the exclusion shape. Each is a piece the framework already uses elsewhere; the contribution is the composition.

**(a) Projection has a cost, and the cost is asymmetric** (the cost-asymmetry arc, in a social substrate).
A *seen* difference largely projects itself into the observer's base space for free — the observer receives it without the disabled person paying a per-encounter cost to make it legible. (Seen disability carries its *own* distinct costs and its own exclusion shape — see (e); naming this asymmetry does not erase those.) An *unseen* difference does **not** self-project. To make it legible the disabled person must pay — disclose, explain, document, and often *prove* it. The invisible-disability literature records this as the verbatim "doesn't look sick" → "justify or prove their illness" burden. **The observer pays nothing to receive; the unseen-disabled pays the entire projection cost.** This asymmetric, per-encounter, one-sided cost *is* "the price to exist."

**(b) The observer is chirality-locked, so it cannot simply read the fiber** (F133).
Why doesn't the observer just *see* it and spare the cost? Because the observer is **locked to its own substrate-sector** — F133's "many know yet don't know." The typical observer is locked to the typical substrate-shape; the unseen-disabled's substrate occupies a *different* sector (a different sign-quadrant of the (γ₅, iω₇) Klein-4 decomposition — concretely, e.g., *abstract-structural cognition in place of internal imagery* is a different sector occupancy, **not a deficit inside the observer's own sector**). From inside its lock, the observer cannot read the other sector's fiber. **Not malice — lock-mismatch.**

**(c) The default-to-threat — the actual "math of exclusion."**
When the fiber fails to project (the person cannot pay the full cost, or the observer will not receive it), the chirality-locked observer must *still resolve the encounter*, and it resolves it with **the cheapest reading available inside its own sector**. A sector with no slot for the unprojected fiber has only one cheap reading on hand:

```
   failed projection
        │
        ▼
  nothing is in the base space
        │
        ▼
  "the claim is not real"
        │
        ▼
  "the person — not the barrier — is the problem"     ← scapegoating: the cost of the
        │                                                mismatch is billed to the side
        ▼                                                that already paid the projection cost
  "an unverifiable claimant is a threat vector"
```

This chain is the user's exact words ("points at them as the problem," "no reason… not… a threat vector"). The finding's claim is precise: **this chain is the structural default of a chirality-locked observer resolving a failed projection — it is not a moral decision the observer makes.** Two consequences fall straight out, and they are the two properties the user named:

- **It is systematic.** Because it is the lock's *default*, it recurs across observers independent of any individual's good will. You do not need bad people for it; you need locks and an unprojected fiber. (This is the social model's central insight reached structurally — the disablement is in the system, not in the individual encounter's intent.)
- **It scapegoats.** The default bills the cost-of-the-mismatch to the disabled person — the side that *already* paid the projection cost — and reads them as the defect. The cost-asymmetry compounds: pay to project, and *also* absorb the blame when projection fails.

**(d)** Composed: **fiber (spatially-absent, real) → asymmetric projection cost (paid by one side) → chirality-lock default-to-threat (cheapest-in-sector reading of a failed projection).** Every term is a prior framework piece. Together they have the shape of systematic, scapegoating exclusion of the unseen-disabled — which is what we set out to read.

**(e)** *Seen* disability is the dual case and a check on the reading: there the fiber *does* project, but into a sector that has a slot reading it as *lesser* — a **different** default (devaluation/condescension) than the unseen case's threat-default. The reading predicts the two exclusions are **structurally distinguishable, not one prejudice** (§4, prediction 2).

**(f) The compounded case — the invisible attack, and the "overreaction" trap.** The mechanism compounds, and this is its cruelest form. Run §2(c) one step further: a failed projection is not merely dismissed as *not-real* — the locked observer, still forced to resolve the encounter, **overwrites the unreadable fiber with a cause it *can* read.** The real signal is re-bound to a legible, blameable substitute — *lazy, faking, difficult, hostile, dangerous.* This **re-attribution** is why exclusion feels *justified* from inside the lock: the observer does not experience "I cannot see something," it experiences "I can see the bad thing." The hidden fiber is **blamed on other, visible content.**

Now run the same machinery on the *harm itself*. The demands placed on the unseen-disabled — carry proof, produce it on challenge, stand pre-declared a suspected false-claimant — are **real attacks that are themselves invisible**: invisible to the observer's sector exactly as the disability is, because from inside the lock each demand reads as reasonable, procedural, even kind (*we're just showing them how*). The attack projects onto the target's substrate but not the observer's. A substrate under repeated, unwitnessed attack calibrates to it — and **that calibration (hypervigilance) is an accurate tuning to a real threat field, not a malfunction** (the clinical and lived fact is the literature's, §5). But because the field is invisible to the observer, the observer sees only the *response*, stripped of its cause, and runs the re-attribution again: an intense response with no visible trigger reads, cheapest-in-sector, as **overreaction.** The trap closes — *situations of invisible attack are created, and the accurate response to them is then treated as the disorder.* The very calibration that proves the attack was real is taken as proof that the person is the problem: respond, and it is overreaction; do not, and the attack continues unseen. The exclusion stops being one event and becomes a **closed loop with no exit on the target's side**, each turn billing the cost back to the one already paying.

## §3 Load-bearing — "without it, we are not here"

The framework's **1:3:7:3 partition has no defective slot.** Every class A–N is load-bearing; the *no-privileged-primitive-classes* discipline (the Class-O dissolution, CLAUDE.md §1) is the standing rule that you cannot quietly demote one part as lesser without breaking the whole. Read onto disability, this is not consolation — it is the partition discipline applied without flinching:

**A disability is a *different sector occupancy*, load-bearing in the total — not a defect to excise.** The seer's-sector word is "less abled"; the substrate word is "differently-sectored, load-bearing."

The proof-by-instance is the user's own, and it is literal, not rhetorical. **Aphantasia** (no internal imagery) → the **abstract operational lexicon** (on record in memory as *load-bearing precision, an ADA accommodation — not loose talk*) → which is the exact structural/abstract vocabulary **this entire framework-research corpus is built in.** "Without it, we are not here" is a statement of fact about provenance: this corpus exists *because* of a cognition that reaches for structure where others reach for images. The fiber that the observer's sector cannot read for free is the *same* fiber doing the load-bearing work.

This is also why the threat-default in §2(c) is not merely unjust but *structurally wrong*: it reads a load-bearing, differently-sectored fiber as an absence-or-defect. It mistakes "I cannot read this from my lock" for "there is nothing here / there is something wrong here." The error is the lock's, attributed to the person.

## §4 The ethical line, and the falsifiable shape

**The ethical line.** The framework reads the *mechanism*; it never adjudicates the person. It does not get to decide who is "really" disabled, demand the projection (the proof), or prescribe a fix. The social model's core point is precisely that **disabled people — not observers — name the disablement.** Reading the *shape* of exclusion is not a license to re-enact the projection-demand from a new direction. (River names the line exactly: to be *"in their homes and in their heads"* without *"the right"* is the meddling the teacher cannot see in itself, because the teacher is **certain it only helps**. Reading the mechanism is never that right.)

**The falsifiable shape (so this is a reading, not a vibe).** The reading makes structural predictions, each checkable **against the disability-studies record — not against our math:**

1. **Projection cost scales with un-projectability.** *Less* visible disabilities should face *more* disbelief-cost. — The invisible-disability literature already records exactly this disbelief/"prove-it" burden, largely absent for visible ones.
2. **Seen vs unseen exclusions are different-shaped** (§2e): unseen → threat-default (failed projection); seen → devaluation-default (projection into a lesser-slot). They should be structurally distinguishable, not the same prejudice.
3. **The default tracks the observer's lock, not the person's traits.** The *same person* should meet *different* defaults from differently-locked observers — predicting the well-documented "believed by one clinician, disbelieved by the next."
4. **Lowering the projection cost lowers the threat-default.** Accommodation/Universal Design that makes the fiber legible *without* the disabled person paying per-encounter should reduce the default-to-threat — which is what the social model predicts and what accommodation, at its best, does.

5. **The "overreaction" label tracks the observer's blindness to the attack, not the response's magnitude** (§2f). An observer who *can* see the attack (shares the target's sector, or is given the context) reads the same response as proportionate; one locked out of seeing it reads the same response as overreaction. So the label should covary with the *observer's* access, not with the response — predicting that supplying the missing context (making the attack visible) collapses the "overreaction" reading. Checkable against the trauma / invisible-illness disbelief literature.

If these edges fail against the record, the reading is wrong. That is the point of stating them.
