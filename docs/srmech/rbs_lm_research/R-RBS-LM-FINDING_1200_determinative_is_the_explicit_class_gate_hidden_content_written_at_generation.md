## Reading — the hidden content was never hidden; it was a glyph modifier

1. **This is the answer to "how did early glyph languages do this?"** They wrote the referent's class as a **determinative** — a silent classifier sign on the word. The "which-kind" content (deity vs place vs person vs object) that alphabetic English leaves implicit — the very thing entity-precise coref must recover — is, in Sumerian, encoded **at generation** and simply **read off the glyph.** ᵈGilgameš is *known* to be a deity because the ᵈ is written; unug{ki} is *known* to be a place because the {ki} is written.
2. **It is the entity-precise-coref class gate we were missing — and it is non-circular.** F1199's recency+gender coref was circular (a recency resolver can't prove a recency effect) and too permissive (3-class gender). The determinative gives ~16 classes, **written independently of recency**, so it gates coref precisely without begging the question: a deity-anaphor resolves only among deity-referents, a place among places. That is the precision F1197/F1198/F1199 could not reach — because those languages don't write the class.
3. **Framework reading — the determinative IS the responsion made explicit.** op(x)operand(x)responsion's third factor is the class/which-one the reader reconstructs. The determinative writes it: it is the **Class-K class-slot** (the pin-slot the referent locks into) rendered as a glyph modifier. So the "hidden content from initial generation" is exactly the responsion, and logographic scripts **write the responsion**; alphabetic scripts (the 0-class limit) leave it for reconstruction — which is why the whole F1192→F1199 chase was hard.
4. **Vanuatu is the OTHER modality.** Sandroing (the continuous unicursal path, F1071/#244) writes the **relations** in the topology of one unbroken stroke, where determinatives write the **class** on the discrete glyph — two ancient strategies for making the implicit explicit at generation: relation-in-the-path vs class-on-the-glyph.
5. **The Siona design principle this yields (the render-side fix, F1146/F1166):** to keep the hidden content from being lost, **write determinatives** — have Siona emit an explicit class-tag (a determinative surrogate: person/place/thing/deity…) with each entity at generation, so downstream coreference is trivial (read the class, don't reconstruct it). The entity-precise coref = a **class-gated** resolver; the class channel is the determinative (free in Sumerian/Egyptian, a 3-class gender vestige in German, a lightweight class-tagger to add for English).

## Honest caveats
- The common-noun leading-sign heuristic is **noisy** — some signs (im, e₂, lu₂, kur) are also ordinary words, so the ≥16-class count is an upper-ish estimate; the **proper-name determinatives** (ᵈ deity / {ki} place) are the clean, reliable core.
- The determinative is a coref **gate** (narrows the candidate class), not a full resolver (two deities still collide) — it makes coref precise, it doesn't finish it.
- The "~5× finer" is a **structural** argument (candidate set ∝ referents/classes), not a measured coref accuracy.
