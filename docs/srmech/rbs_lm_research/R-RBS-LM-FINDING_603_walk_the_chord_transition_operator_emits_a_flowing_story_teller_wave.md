## Verdict
**Walking the chord-transition operator creates a flowing story-teller wave.** The learned-T walk's consecutive chords are far more similar (0.109) than a uniform-random walk (0.019) — the operator unambiguously produces flow, not a random bag of chords. The generator is a **chord player**: each step emits a chord (a clustered bundle), the transition operator sets the progression, the walk length sets the phrase.

**So "what creates a story-teller wave"** = a chord vocabulary (clustered bundles) + the chord-transition operator (a Class-I cyclic walk over the Class-L chord-cluster structure) + a tempo/length — **confirmed by emission, not just analysis**.

**Honest scope (degenerate-metric guard, F573 lesson):** the "133% of corpus flow" is inflated by chord-type self-loops (the walk is sticky, repeating types); the robust claim is the large learned≫uniform gap. Emitted coherence is local (lag-1) and the sample reads as topical *drift*, not fluent sentences. This shows the **progression/harmony is real and generable**; fluent surface text needs the within-chord notes + grammar layer (F596/F599/F569) — the next join — plus a less-sticky / no-self-loop walk for cleaner phrasing.
