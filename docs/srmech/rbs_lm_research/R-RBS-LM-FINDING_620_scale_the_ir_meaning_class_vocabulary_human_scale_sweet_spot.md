# R-RBS-LM Finding 620 (scaling the IR meaning-class vocabulary — the K-sweep finds a human-scale sweet spot (K≈64, dozens), then the F222 capacity ceiling reverses it; the learned vocabulary converges on the order of the attested human meaning-class systems) — **Discussed + measured how the kernel's Layer-1 IR (the meaning-class / σ_B set) should scale. Design (no-privileged-language F398): the IR vocabulary is LEARNED from co-occurrence (F602 unsupervised clusters, scales with corpus, no language privileged), GROUNDED as a cross-check (not source, F581) in the attested human meaning-class systems (~28 Egyptian Gardiner determinative categories F582/F585; ~45 WordNet supersenses), with a natural ceiling from F222 capacity. Swept K (the IR vocabulary size) on the F602 pseudoword WSD task (Simple English Wikipedia, 30 pseudowords, 9000 occurrences, held-out): single-stream baseline (no meaning-class) 48.0%; coupled (E×B) accuracy K=4 67.4% → K=8 68.1% → K=16 68.9% → K=32 71.1% → K=64 71.8% (peak, +23.8%, backoff 8%) → K=128 70.7% (DECLINING, backoff 16%). So the IR vocabulary has a SWEET SPOT, not "more is always better": accuracy rises with K (more classes = sharper disambiguation) until BACKOFF (sparser cells per class than the corpus supports — the F222 capacity / F602 effect) flattens then REVERSES it. The peak (K≈64, dozens) is HUMAN-SCALE — the same order as the attested human meaning-class systems (~28 Gardiner, ~45 supersenses) — so the no-privileged learned classes converge on the order of the human meaning-classes they should be grounded against (a validation + the natural IR size). More corpus would push the sweet spot up; the SHAPE (rise-then-flatten-then-decline) is the finding. NET: "scale the IR vocabulary" = learn dozens-to-low-hundreds of co-occurrence classes (F602), ground them by mapping to the attested determinative/supersense categories (interpretability + cross-check), content-address each (bit-exact, attestable) — NOT thousands of English senses (F398); the F222 capacity ceiling makes the IR vocabulary naturally human-scale.**

## Verdict
**The IR vocabulary has a human-scale sweet spot.** Accuracy rises with K (more meaning-classes = sharper) until the F222 capacity ceiling bites — cells get sparser than the corpus supports, backoff climbs (16% at K=128), and accuracy *declines*. The peak (K≈64, dozens) is the **same order as the attested human meaning-class systems** (~28 Gardiner determinatives, ~45 supersenses) — so the no-privileged learned classes converge on the order of the human meaning-classes they should be grounded against. More corpus would push the peak up; the rise-then-decline *shape* is the finding.

**So "scale the IR vocabulary" =** learn dozens-to-low-hundreds of co-occurrence classes (F602), ground them by mapping to the attested determinative/supersense categories (cross-check + interpretability, F581), and content-address each (bit-exact, attestable) — **not** thousands of English senses (F398). The capacity ceiling (F222) makes the IR vocabulary naturally human-scale. **Next:** ground the learned classes → map each to its nearest Gardiner determinative (a named, interpretable IR vocabulary), then run the full English↔IR↔ASL loop (F616) on the scaled vocab. Favored not privileged (F398); held open (F394).
