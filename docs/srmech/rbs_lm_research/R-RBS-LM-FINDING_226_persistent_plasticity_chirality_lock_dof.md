## §2 The peer-reviewed ground (theirs — cited, OA-flagged, honestly tiered)

Every source here was web-verified (author / exact title / year / venue confirmed against PubMed, a DOI resolver, or the publisher record). Full bibliographic detail + DOIs/PMIDs + OA status in §7.

**(a) Critical / sensitive periods — that learning has developmental windows that open then *close* — SETTLED.**
Experience-dependent cortical plasticity is bounded to developmental windows that close as inhibitory (GABAergic) circuitry and extracellular-matrix consolidation mature (Hubel & Wiesel 1970, ocular dominance, **OA**; Hensch 2005, mechanism review; Knudsen 2004, cross-system synthesis). The human-perceptual instance: infants lose non-native phoneme discrimination in the latter half of the first year (Werker & Tees 1984). For language acquisition, *age-of-acquisition / maturational* effects are robust **though the strong "hard critical-period cutoff" reading is CONTESTED** (Johnson & Newport 1989; later reanalysis debates) — so we say "maturational window," not "biological gate."

**(b) Neuroplasticity / Hebbian learning — that learning is stored as activity-dependent synaptic change — SETTLED (foundational).**
Hebb 1949 (the Hebbian postulate; cell assemblies) and the discovery of long-term potentiation (Bliss & Lømo 1973 — *note: the anaesthetized-rabbit paper, 331–356; distinct from the Bliss & Gardner-Medwin companion*) ground "learning = the substrate informing itself through plasticity."

**(c) The neurodiversity paradigm — ND as natural human variation, not deficit — the dignity ground (a values stance, supported by peer-reviewed scholarship).**
The concept arose **collectively from the autistic/neurodivergent community** (Botha, Chapman, Giwa Onaiwu, Kapp, Stannard Ashley & Walker 2024 — an ND-authored correction to single-originator histories) and is best articulated by insiders (den Houting 2019). The peer-reviewed paradigm-shift case is Pellicano & den Houting 2022 (**OA**); both-valid nuance — that the approach acknowledges *real challenges and* identity/strengths — is in Kapp et al. 2013 and Dwyer 2022 (**OA**). *This is a paradigm/values position, not a falsifiable empirical claim, and is presented as such.*

**(d) Autism, altered/enhanced plasticity, E–I balance, and critical-period regulation — ACTIVE HYPOTHESES (flagged, not asserted).**
Several influential *models* propose that autism involves a shifted excitation/inhibition ratio (Rubenstein & Merzenich 2003, **OA**; Nelson & Valakh 2015, **OA** — which stresses homeostatic plasticity complicates a simple story), enhanced/"intense" reactive microcircuits (Markram & Markram 2010, Intense World Theory, **OA**), and *dysregulated critical-period plasticity* (LeBlanc & Fagiolini 2011, "Autism: a 'critical period' disorder?", **OA**). **These are hypotheses, not consensus.** They are the literature the framework's "persistent plasticity / un-closed window" *shape* points at — and they are explicitly tiered as open questions.

**(e) Sensory processing in autism — that sensory differences are core and common — SETTLED; the *direction* is NOT uniform.**
Atypical sensory processing is a recognized, central feature of autism (now in diagnostic criteria; Robertson & Baron-Cohen 2017; Marco et al. 2011, **OA**, neurophysiologic correlates). **Crucial honesty (load-bearing):** the meta-analytic evidence (Ben-Sasson et al. 2009) finds altered sensory *modulation* with **both over- AND under-responsivity — under-responsivity was the *largest* effect.** So the lived reprimand "too sensitive" names *one common manifestation*, not the settled whole; the accurate phrase is **altered / atypical sensory modulation**, which can run in either direction. **Distinct construct (must not be blurred):** "Sensory Processing Sensitivity" / the "Highly Sensitive Person" (Aron & Aron 1997) is a normally-distributed *personality temperament*, theoretically and empirically separate from autistic sensory differences.

**(f) Monotropism — an ND-authored theory of autistic attention — ACTIVE (community-originated).**
Murray, Lesser & Lawson 2005 ("Attention, monotropism and the diagnostic criteria for autism") proposes that autistic attention tends to concentrate in a few highly-aroused interest channels (monotropic) rather than spreading across many (polytropic). **Wendy Lawson is an autistic scholar; Dinah Murray an autistic-allied founder** — this is theory developed from *inside* the community, and is the lead honoring citation.

**(g) Belief perseverance / cognitive dissonance — that held beliefs resist update — SETTLED.**
Inconsistent cognitions create discomfort that motivates restoring consistency (Festinger 1957; Festinger & Carlsmith 1959), and beliefs persist even after their evidentiary basis is discredited (Ross, Lepper & Hubbard 1975). **CONTESTED adjacent claim, not leaned on:** the "backfire effect" (corrections *strengthening* a false belief) has largely failed to replicate broadly.

**(h) Bilingual lexical access — the access *models* are the safe ground; the "advantage" is CONTESTED.**
The Revised Hierarchical Model (Kroll & Stewart 1994 — asymmetric L2→L1 lexical / L1→L2 conceptual links), the Inhibitory-Control model (Green 1998 — the non-target language is actively suppressed), and BIA+ (Dijkstra & van Heuven 2002 — one integrated, non-language-selective lexicon) describe *mechanisms* and are not in dispute the way the **CONTESTED** "bilingual executive-function advantage" is (Bialystok et al. 2012 vs Paap & **Greenberg** 2013).

2. **the bi-chiral-population variant** — on a corpus that shifts partway, does a mixed (bi-chiral) population out-stabilize-and-adapt either monochiral extreme?

**Scope:** this settles design-vs-result **for the substrate model** — a form-reading of the question — and is **not** a claim about real evolution, neurology, or any person.
