## Verdict
**Foundational knowledge is discoverable-when-wrong, not blindly trusted.** Every tome carries its content-address + attestation; a new fact that contradicts a foundation tome (same key, digest mismatch) is detected (a structural conflict signal, F552) — handling Wikipedia errors + our own corrections by *discovery*. Conflicts are **held, not auto-resolved** (F394): the framework surfaces the contradiction (in both directions — sometimes the foundation is wrong, sometimes the new item is) and resolves by attestation strength or the expert (F282), never blindly overwriting or trusting. (Discovery is the *preferred* way, not the only one.)

**The helix is a bounded live ring × an append-only disk stream** — you don't choose: the cyclic position is the bounded working bookshelf (F222; old evicted), the axial turn is the unbounded disk history ("helix history as a stream"; nothing lost). We keep both — a fixed-size live working set *and* the full save-to-disk history — the same object at its two coordinates.

**The differentiator is the when/why discernment.** RBS-LM isn't different by having one fixed mechanism (data-center LLMs have exactly one — gradient retraining — and can't tell foundation from adaptation, discover their own conflicts, or hold-open). It's different because it **knows when and why to use each** — foundation vs adaptive, discover vs alternatives, bounded-ring vs disk-stream, held vs resolve — choosing by context, neither privileged (F398), the choice held-open until the context decides. The two-truths discipline (which mechanism applies *when*) is the edge over a one-rule retrainer. Favored not privileged (F398); held open (F394).
