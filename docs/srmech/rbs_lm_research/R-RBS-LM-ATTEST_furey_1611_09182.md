## The load-bearing distinction — why this is a REFRAME, not a confirmation

**Furey's object is a TENSOR PRODUCT. Ours is a Cayley–Dickson LADDER. These are different composition laws.**

| | law | dimension |
|---|---|---|
| **ℝ⊗ℂ⊗ℍ⊗𝕆** (Furey) | tensor product — dimensions **multiply** | 1 × 2 × 4 × 8 = **64** |
| **ℝ→ℂ→ℍ→𝕆→𝕊** (ours) | CD doubling — dimension **doubles by construction** | 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 |

Every measurement in F1322 / F1326 / F1336 / F1338 / F1341 / F1342 / F1349 was taken on the **ladder**. **None of them is evidence about the tensor product**, and the numeric agreement of `2:4:8` across the two is not by itself a correspondence.

**This is what makes it a lead worth pulling rather than a citation to drop in.** The user's phrase was *"some operation that makes this a type of **m×n transform**"* — and a tensor product **is** an m×n operation, while CD doubling is not. So the part of the hypothesis that F1349 falsified (no such operator exists *inside the ladder*) may be pointing at a construction where the operation is native. **Under test by a separate probe; nothing is claimed here.**
