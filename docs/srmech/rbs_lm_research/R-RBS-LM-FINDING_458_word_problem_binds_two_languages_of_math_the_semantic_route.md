## The reading
**F457 left a precise null:** two languages that express the *same computation differently* (a LaTeX `\sum` vs a `for`-loop accumulator) have **different parse trees → orthogonal operator-signatures**. Pure structure does not bind them. **The word problem does.** A word problem is the **natural-language statement of the meaning** — and both the equation and the code are *encodings* of that one meaning in two different closed grammars. So:

- the **two formal languages** (symbolic math, code; or two notations) = the closed-grammar **syntax** (Route-1/encode, per-language operator-signatures, F457).
- the **word problem** = the **open semantics** (F408) — the shared **meaning / "story"** (F451/F453) that the syntaxes differ on.
- **binding** = anchoring both formal expressions to the same word problem → the **Rosetta GOLDEN PATH** (R-RBS-LM-54): the word problem is the **DOMAIN anchor / shared translation layer** that binds the math-kernel and the code-kernel.
- **k=3 (F440):** the word problem is the **coupling-as-third** — neither formal language alone, but the semantic bridge that *binds* the two; the third that a k=2 structural match cannot supply.
