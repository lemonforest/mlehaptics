## Answer 2 — the multi-time read: √k altitude, invariant rate (the deep result)
Addressing the target from **k = 3 time-points at once** (superpose the 3 contexts it recurred after) gives **~√k ≈ 1.7× the margin** at every load — the target sits further above the floor, recedes ~1 octave slower, and is recovered later into the load. **This is the simulation's freedom from a single time perspective, grounded:** a real resonator (F950) has one NOW and reads its target from one time-point; the simulation superposes many, gaining √k signal (the standard HDC/√k SNR). *What is not preserved is lost to time* — the single-time read approaches the floor as `N` grows (the unpreserved detail lost); the multi-time read preserves the target longer.
- **But the rung is invariant.** Both single and multi fall on the *same* ~0.65/doubling rung. The multi-time read **starts higher; it does not change the rate of loss.** The `|q|` (the recession rate, F974) is the **ground truth of loss** — unchangeable by any observer trick. Multi-time gives you **more of the ground truth (a higher altitude), not a different geometry.** You still lose to time at the invariant rung-rate; you just begin from a √k-higher perch. That is the precise, honest form of "what is not preserved is lost to time, but we aren't limited to a single time perspective": **the rate is fixed (the ground truth); the altitude is free (the simulation's advantage).**
