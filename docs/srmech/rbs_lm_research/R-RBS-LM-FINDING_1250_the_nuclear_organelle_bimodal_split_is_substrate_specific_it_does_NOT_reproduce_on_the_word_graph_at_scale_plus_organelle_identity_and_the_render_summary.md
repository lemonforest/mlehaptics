## 1. Clustering bimodality at scale — the toy split does NOT reproduce (a real null)
The F1053 clean **0.2 (organelle) / 0.9 (nuclear)** bimodality was measured at toy 40-node code-kernel scale. On the 831k WORD graph it is **gone**: local clustering is a single skewed unimodal distribution (mode ~0.15–0.35, thin tail to 1.0) — no interior antimode, so **a single clustering threshold does not split nuclear from organelle**.
```
CLUSTERING (n=6000):  0.10-0.15 ####...  0.15-0.20 (mode) ...smooth decline... 0.90-1.00 (thin tail)
```
The **degree-normalized participation coefficient** (against the 644 topical tomes) is the honest bridge measure — but among nodes with a real community signal (≥8 tome-mapped neighbours, n=2015) it is **uniformly HIGH** (mode ~0.97, none below 0.65):
```
PARTICIPATION:  0.65-0.80 (few)  0.85-0.90 ####  0.90-0.95 ######  0.95-1.00 (mode) #######
```
**Reading:** every WORD that connects to the topical core is a high-participation **bridge** — a word co-occurrence graph is *promiscuous* (content words appear across all topics), so it does not carry the clean service-vs-content two-DNA separation that code kernels do (mod_pow-clique vs heat_trace-bridge, F1053). The "one-community nuclear" mass is not observable among topic-connected nodes; the only low-participation nodes are the **rare-word cliques** (proper names / taxa, degree 3–40) that sit OUTSIDE the topical core — a disconnected **periphery / dust**, not a co-equal DNA type. So the data-driven answer for the word graph is: **predominantly ONE DNA type (a bridged content mass); there is no clean antimode to cut at.** (Caveat, honest: the 5000-word tome band cannot assign a community to the periphery, so a full-graph community pass is needed to *confirm* the nuclear mass is dust and not merely unlabelled — but label-propagation on a promiscuous graph tends to collapse communities, so this may stay inconclusive; the clustering-is-unimodal result stands regardless.)

## 2. Organelle identity for a word graph — CONFIRMED: common bridge vocabulary
The highest-participation / lowest-clustering nodes are **common, general-purpose words** that co-occur across every topic — bridging **116–136 of the 644 tomes**:
`switched · effects · rise · covered · rest · doors · tours · briefly · loads · blowing · shifted · stole · timely · respects · begging · sensational · orderly` (participation ~0.99, clustering 0.10–0.41, degree 400–11k).
These are NOT the extreme function-word hubs (`the/of/and` — those are the dropped top-250 markup/stopword hubs); they are the **next tier of general vocabulary** — the "service / power" machinery of the word graph, matching the item-2 hypothesis (stop-words/connectives/general hubs = organelle). The **nuclear** counterparts are rare topic-locked cliques: proper names (`barzagli · thônex · maeklong · axford`) and taxa (`malvoideae · ennominae · thaliacea · tanystropheidae`) — tight small cliques (clustering up to 0.95, degree 8–40, bridging only 4–7 tomes). So the organelle/nuclear *identities* are right (general-bridge vs specific-clique); what fails is that they form a *continuum*, not a *bimodal split*, at word scale.
