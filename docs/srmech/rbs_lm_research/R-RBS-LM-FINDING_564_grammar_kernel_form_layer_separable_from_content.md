## Result (6612 sentences, length 3–14)
- **(1) function-word scaffold (top):** the, of, is, in, and, a, are, to, it, this, for, they, was, that, by, called, or, on, as, from …

## Verdict
**The form layer is real and separable.** The corpus's grammar decomposes into a function-word **scaffold** (35% of tokens), a sentence-**length law** (median 10), and a **soft POS** from function-word context — pure FORM, independent of *which* content fills it (F311). **Honest snag:** function-word-*literal* frames don't capture reusable templates (function words vary in position, so the common exact skeletons are content-only) — the reusable structure is **POS-class + local scaffold transitions**, not fixed slots. So the **renderer (step 2) must weave the scaffold into the content stream** (function-word-in-context), not fill positional slots.

**The RBS-LM ↔ gen-1-LLM difference (the user's prompt).** A gen-1 LLM **entangles** content and form in one learned next-token distribution — attention over positions does grammar *implicitly*, inseparable from content. RBS-LM **separates** them: the story manifold is **content** (F538–F562), this grammar kernel is **form**. That separation *is* the architectural inversion (F311/F50) — and it's *why* "sentence structure on top of the story" is even a well-posed move here: the two layers exist independently, so one can be put on the other. Favored not privileged (F398); held open (F394).
