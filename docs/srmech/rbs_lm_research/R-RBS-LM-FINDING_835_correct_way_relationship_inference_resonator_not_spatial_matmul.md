## The guardrail, stated first (the drift-alarm)
Relationship inference is **addressing**, not arithmetic over dense embeddings. Concretely, per step it:
- takes candidates from the **relationship graph** (the legal successors / the bound slots) — a handful, not the vocab;
- resolves by **resonance/unbind + cleanup** against just those candidates.
So its cost is **O(legal-relationship-candidates × iterations)**, never **O(vocab × D)** dense matmul. **There is no "hash rate" to compare** — the computation is structurally different from GPU/spatial inference (matmul over embeddings, throughput in tokens/sec). **Watch-for list (any one = we've drifted back to imitating spatial inference):** (a) benchmarking tokens/sec or "hash rate"; (b) "is it as fast / as accurate as a transformer"; (c) scoring the full vocab densely each step; (d) treating recall as exact-readback (F834: that's retrieval, not inference). Evaluate instead by **groundedness** (every candidate is a stored relationship), **factorization convergence** (does the resonator settle), and the **inference signature** (grounded recombination, not verbatim, not invented).

## What the literature says the correct method is (verified)
1. **Relations are bindings; inference is unbinding.** Kanerva's "what is the *dollar* of Mexico → peso" is solved by binding country⊗role facts and *unbinding* the analogy — relational inference IS bind/unbind, not similarity in a spatial embedding. — Kanerva, P. (2009). *Hyperdimensional Computing: An Introduction to Computing in Distributed Representation with High-Dimensional Random Vectors.* **Cognitive Computation 1(2):139–159.** doi:10.1007/s12559-009-9009-8.
2. **A sequence is a bound+bundled holographic state.** Trajectory-association encodes "abc" as `S = a + b⊗k + c⊗k²` (successive bindings to a key); the items are recovered by unbinding. This is exactly srmech's `encode_context` (positional role-filler `pos_key⊗token`, bundled) — the relationship-faithful context state. — Plate, T.A. (1995). *Holographic Reduced Representations.* **IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks 6(3):623–641.** doi:10.1109/72.377968.
3. **The principled multi-factor read is a resonator network.** Given a superposition that is a *product of several unknown codevectors*, recover all factors by **iterated unbind + cleanup-in-superposition** (search in superposition, nonlinear dynamics) — convergent and far higher-capacity than one-shot unbind or least-squares. — Frady, E.P., Kent, S.J., Olshausen, B.A., & Sommer, F.T. (2020). *Resonator Networks, 1: An Efficient Solution for Factoring High-Dimensional, Distributed Representations of Data Structures.* **Neural Computation 32(12):2311–2331.** doi:10.1162/neco_a_01331. (Survey context: Kleyko, Rachkovskij, Osipov & Rahimi, *A Survey on Hyperdimensional Computing aka Vector Symbolic Architectures*, arXiv:2112.15424.)

## How srmech.rbs_lm already maps onto this (the 1-factor case)
`learn` builds the two relationship structures and `next_token_distribution` does the read:
- **relationship graph** `next_after[a]` = which tokens legally follow `a` (discrete adjacency; Class-I/L) → the candidate set;
- **holographic memory** `M = bundle_i bind(encode_context(window_i), next_i)` (capacity-bounded) → the associative store;
- **read** = `probe = bind(M, encode_context(context))` (one **unbind** of the context role) → `sim_k4_batch(probe, legal_candidates)` (cleanup against the codebook) → softmax.
This is precisely **one resonator step** (unbind the known factor, clean up the unknown) over the graph-restricted codebook — the correct method when there is a *single* unknown (the next token) and a clean context. **It already scores only the legal candidates, not the vocab** — guardrail-clean by construction.

## The "correct way" upgrade for richer relationships (multi-factor)
When more than one factor is unknown — recovering a full relationship tuple from a bound fact, disentangling several superposed continuations, the "relationships of relationships" (F55/F77), analogical hops — the one-shot unbind is not enough; the correct operator is the **resonator**: iterate `unbind factor i → clean up against codebook i → re-bind` until the factorization converges. This is **the iterated form of the project's own `klein4_unbundle` (F822/§54) / deconvolution (F820)**, with `sim_k4_batch` as the cleanup and the per-clump candidate set as the codebook — i.e. it **composes existing srmech ops** (no hand-rolled math): `klein4_unbind` + `sim_k4_batch` cleanup, iterated to convergence. Whether to ship a first-class `resonator`/`factorize` op in srmech is the natural §-ask (composes §54).
