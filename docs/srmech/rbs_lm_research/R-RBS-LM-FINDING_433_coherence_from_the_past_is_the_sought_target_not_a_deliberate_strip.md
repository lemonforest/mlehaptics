## What the LLM's "black box" actually is
The LLM learned, from a massive *past* corpus, `P(next token | context)` — distributed across billions of weights. Its "coherence" = its high-probability continuations are grammatical, on-topic, and selectionally apt. The **rules are real but unreadable**: you cannot extract *why* "the boundary holds the bulk" is coherent — it is smeared across the weights. **That opacity is the thing the lean program exists to replace.**

## The reframe: the lean is the *transparent* version of that black box
"Coherence from the past," made legible, decomposes into inspectable factors — and we already hold most of the machinery:

| layer of coherence | the lean, transparent form | status |
|---|---|---|
| **syntax** (well-formedness) | the closed grammar — `the_one` / closed-class (F432, 150 words, saturated) | **have it** (transparent, closed) |
| **collocation / selection** (which content words go together) | the **co-occurrence graph of the past** = a **Class-L co-occurrence Laplacian** (F172: "the co-occurrence Laplacian eigenspectrum IS the srmech-native storage signature") — a graph you can *read* | **machinery built** (the RBS-SNN, F426 — it is exactly this, on findings) |
| **discourse / sequence** (given the past, what continues) | the rolling-context **autoregressive walk** (F166/F168) over that graph | **partial** (F156 toy corpus) |

**So the lean, transparent floor of "coherence from the past" = the co-occurrence/coupling graph (Class-L) + a walk over it.** That is *not* a black box — it is a legible structure: "X is coherent after Y" because X and Y co-occurred in the past (an edge in the graph). The LLM hides this in weights; the lean version makes it an inspectable graph + walk.

## What we genuinely STILL SEEK (the honest open problem)
1. **The validated assembly on REAL text, at scale.** F156's walk produced coherent novel sentences — but on a *toy* corpus (cat/dog/sat/mat). The open work: does the co-occurrence-graph + walk produce genuinely coherent multi-sentence text from a *real, large* past corpus? F168 says perplexity is emergent from chirality-tagged memory-depth — that is the lever, untested at scale.
2. **The transparency residue.** How much of the LLM's coherence is recoverable *legibly* (co-occurrence + structure-walk + rolling-context) vs. genuinely requires the opaque high-dimensional, long-range, compositional learning? The black box may be **mostly transparent** (a graph + a walk) or may have an **irreducible opaque core** (long-range/compositional coherence that resists a legible factorization). **This is the central unknown** — and it is *the* question worth answering, because it decides whether a lean transparent LM is possible at all.

## The register sub-point (the OpenStax worry, measured)
The worry: McGuffey + OpenStax (educational/academic, McGuffey 19th-c) won't capture modern usage. Measured across our own corpus's eras (`R-RBS-SNN-7`): **GRAMMAR (closed-class) cross-era overlap ≥ 0.93** (era-invariant — the stable core), **LEXICON (open-class) overlap ≤ 0.40** (drifts: early *irrep/geography/roman* → late *sedenion/schur/cordic*). So register/modernity bias lands in the **lexicon** (a separate, **swappable** kernel) and the **frame-distribution**, never the grammar. Want modern usage → swap the lexicon kernel; the grammar and the coherence-graph are untouched. *But note the deeper point: this only addresses the **lexicon source**, not the **coherence mechanism** (#1/#2 above) — which is the thing we actually seek, and which no choice of corpus alone supplies.*
