## Verdict on the three claims
**(1) RBS-NN is the irreducible structure for learning — YES, sharpened: irreducible as a TWO-TIER LAYERING, not a monolith.** Learning is not one thing; it has two floors with two different irreducible units (F308/F309):
- **Tier 1 — cellular storage / memory** (habituation). Irreducible floor = the **aneural single cell** (F308). Needs no neuron.
- **Tier 2 — neural contingency-detection / intent** (operant/associative). Irreducible floor = the **single neuron** (F309, *Aplysia* B51). Needs the action↔outcome partition.
RBS-NN's two-tier architecture (F119 discrete-cyclic Tier 1 + synaptic-NN Tier 2; F120 Class K = the Tier1↔Tier2 bridge) **is exactly this structure.** So the biology bottoms out *at* RBS-NN's two tiers: it is the irreducible *layering* that spans both kinds of learning — you cannot do intent-learning with less than Tier 2, nor memory with less than Tier 1, and neither tier reduces further. (Honest caveat: habituation-only learning needs *only* Tier 1 — so RBS-NN is irreducible for the *full span*, while each tier is independently floored.)

**(2) Includes jellies without central brains — YES, strongly (brain-agnostic by construction, F118).** Cnidarians have **no central brain** — a diffuse nerve net + nerve ring(s) + rhopalia (Satterlie 2011, "Do jellyfish have central nervous systems?"). Yet the box jellyfish *Tripedalia cystophora* (4 rhopalia, ~1000 neurons each) shows **associative learning localized to the rhopalial nervous system** (Bielecki, Nielsen, Nachman & Garm 2023, *Curr. Biol.*). So Tier 2 = "neural contingency-detection," which a **decentralized rhopalium/nerve-ring satisfies without centralization.** RBS-NN never required a brain (F118: "NN is vertebrate-centric" → RBS-NN generalizes); the jellyfish is a Tier-2 organism *without* a central brain. Confirmed.

**(3) Medusa→polyp destroyed neurons — DIRECTIONALLY YES, but "destroyed" is imprecise.** Best-attested:
- **Turritopsis dohrnii reverse development (medusa→cyst→polyp):** the medusa peptidergic nervous system (RFamide+/GLWamide+ neurons) is **"almost entirely dismantled"** at the cyst stage — neurons "barely or no longer detectable" (Matsumoto, Piraino & Miglietta 2019, *G3*, OA). So the medusa-grade neural tier **is** lost. **But** the mechanism is **transdifferentiation + apoptosis + asymmetric division through a dedifferentiated cyst** — a whole-body reorganization — *not* a clean apoptotic deletion of neurons, and the **per-neuron fate (apoptosis vs transdifferentiation) is UNRESOLVED** in the primary literature (cited to unpublished observations).
- **The cleanest "neurons apoptotically destroyed + rebuilt" is the FORWARD larval→polyp metamorphosis** (Nematostella, Hydractinia) — TUNEL-confirmed apoptosis of larval neurons, polyp nerve net rebuilt de novo from interstitial stem cells (Zang & Nakanishi 2020; Plickert 2011; Bradshaw 2020).
- **Polyp→medusa strobilation (Aurelia)** is *de novo addition* (new rhopalia/nerve-rings; polyp neurons largely **retained + supplemented**), not destruction.

## The load-bearing result — Tier 2 is SEPARABLE, and rides on the Tier-1 stem substrate
This is the real prize for the framework: cnidarians are a **living demonstration that the neural intent-tier can be dismantled and rebuilt while the organism persists on its cellular substrate.**
- The continuity is at the **stem-cell / lineage level (interstitial i-cells / epithelial neural progenitors)**, NOT at the level of individual neurons. Hydractinia: larval neurons apoptosed, polyp net rebuilt from surviving i-cells. Hydra: nerve net regenerated from scratch in ~48 h from i-cells. Turritopsis: the cyst is "a cluster of poorly differentiated cells" — the organism persists as a **largely aneuronal cellular mass** and **re-expresses** a nervous system.
- **This is F309's ladder made flesh:** memory/Tier-1 lives in the persisting cellular (stem) substrate; intent/Tier-2 (neurons) is the **add-on that can be removed and regrown.** And it is another **frame-retreat (F307):** the neural tier = the droppable *filler*; the stem/cellular substrate = the persisting *anchor*.
- **Honest caveat (both tiers):** it is NOT "delete neurons, keep everything else fixed" — the neural tier is dismantled *as part of a whole-body remodel*, not uniquely excised; and **no study has shown zero-neuron → full reconstitution with single-cell lineage tracing across medusa→polyp.** So "separable" holds at the *cell-identity-vs-stem-substrate* level, not as clean independent removal.
