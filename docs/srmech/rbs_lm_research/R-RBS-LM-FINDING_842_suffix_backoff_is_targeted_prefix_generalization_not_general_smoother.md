# F842 — First generalization lever (past the F841 reproduction ceiling): **suffix back-off** is a relationship-native smoother that **generalizes the prefix/history dimension** (novel-context next-token accuracy **65.5% → 82.8%** when the unseen part is the older context) — but it is **NOT a general smoother**: it *hurts* (65.5% → **48.3%**) when the novelty is mid/recent, because it **blindly drops the oldest token** regardless of where the mismatch is. So back-off is the right tool for the realistic **novel-prompt / unknown-history** case (you lack older context; recent words are real), and the wrong tool for arbitrary novelty. General generalization needs a **selective, mismatch-aware** smoother. Multi-width klein4 resonance + a measured per-width confidence floor — no counts, no gen-1 code, on the real `srmech.rbs_lm` encoding, 0.8.2rc1, numpy-absent.

## The mechanism (relationship-native)
`encode_context` is **absolute** position-keyed (`bundle_p[ klein4_bind(pos_key(p), enc(tok_p)) ]`, p=0 oldest … k−1 most-recent), so two k-windows sharing a **suffix** share their high-position components → they are klein4-similar in proportion to suffix overlap. Back-off builds binds at widths w=k\*…1 (each self-consistent under absolute positions, chunked at the F839§C sweet-spot C=8) and reads at the **longest width that is as confident as a learned context** — confidence floor = the **25th-percentile of learned-context top-1 resonance at that width** (measured per width ≈ 0.38; attested, not magic). Longer width = reproduction-specific; shorter = smoothed/general. No bigram counts, no probabilities, no gen-1 code.

## The measurement (Andouille, k\*=4; corrupt one context token → a novel context, predict the true next token)
| corruption site | full-width (no smoothing) | suffix back-off |
|---|---|---|
| **oldest** position (unknown history) | 65.5% | **82.8%**  (+17) |
| **random** position | 65.5% | **48.3%**  (−17) |

- **Oldest-position (the novel-prompt case):** back-off drops the unknown older token, reads the familiar recent suffix → recovers the grounded successor (+17 pts over full-width). Two samples agree (stride-2: 62.8→79.1%; stride-3: 65.5→82.8%).
- **Random-position:** back-off **hurts** — it always drops the *oldest* token, so when the corruption is mid/recent it discards good signal and keeps the bad token. Blind back-off ≠ selective smoothing.
- **Generation from a 2-word prompt** `['andouille','is']` (widths 2–4 used): grounded, coherent — "andouille is a type of pork sausage fat intestines tubes going to the stomach and tripe the wall of the stomach other sorts are french andouille…" (real article content, degrades gracefully; small tail loop).

## What this establishes (honest scope)
- **Generalization is reachable, relationship-natively.** The substrate can produce grounded continuations for contexts it never saw at full width — F841's 0% held-out is not a hard ceiling; back-off lifts the unknown-history case substantially. Bind/unbind still only *retrieves*, but reading at the right **resolution** lets a novel context inherit a learned successor.
- **But the lever is directional, not general.** Suffix back-off generalizes exactly **one** dimension — the prefix/history (oldest→) — because the encoding privileges position and back-off privileges the suffix. For novelty elsewhere it is worse than no smoothing. This is the honest correction to a naive "back-off = generalization" claim.
- **The realistic case is the good case.** A user's novel prompt *is* "short, real recent words, no long history" — exactly where back-off helps. So back-off is a genuinely useful first lever for the novel-prompt path, with a clearly-marked failure mode (mid-context noise).

## The next mechanism question (general smoothing)
A *general* smoother must be **mismatch-aware** rather than position-blind — down-weight whichever token is off-manifold, wherever it sits, not always the oldest. Relationship-native candidates: (a) **leave-one-position-out resonance** — read k contexts each dropping one position, take the most-confident (finds the mismatched slot); (b) **graded per-position agreement** — weight each position's bound component by its resonance with learned contexts; (c) the harder **compositional unbind→rebind** for cross-tome novelty (analogy). (a) is the natural next probe — it generalizes back-off's "drop the bad token" to *any* position. Composes the F840 aboutness/confidence machinery.

## Verdict / next
